DIGITAL PEDAL CALIBRATION

Run from the repository root:
  npm run calibrate:pedals -- --output=artifacts/digital-pedals/report.json
  npm run test:digital-pedals
  node tools/vehicle-validation/calibrate-digital-pedals.mjs --help

Scope
  Original saved cars: RTG-001, CT-86, BUSE-019, PZ-24.
  All eight selectable surfaces from RACE_SURFACES, using production Track State
  profiles and the saved per-wheel tire definitions, dimensions, and pressures.
  Snow is 25.4 mm. Wet surfaces retain their authored water/contamination defaults.
  Automatic transmission. Digital throttle and brake assistance enabled.
  ABS and traction control tested together off and on; stability control off.
  Physics fixed at 120 Hz; button shaping at 60 Hz; virtual driver decisions 10 Hz.

Course stages (isolated starts so a bad launch cannot invalidate later stages)
  1. 402.336 m (quarter-mile) launch, then hold service brake to below 0.5 m/s.
     A binary steering driver maintains the straight centerline.
  2. Separate 60 mph stop for like-for-like braking comparisons.
  3. Level slalom, 220 m: six alternating gates, 30 m spacing, +/-3 m offset.
  4. Same slalom, uphill 7%.
  5. Same slalom, downhill 7%.
  Slaloms begin at 10 m/s; the driver uses on/off throttle/brake around that speed.
  Contact heights follow each wheel's actual position and the grade. These are
  uniform analytical test surfaces, not visual/editor tracks or imported roads.

Inputs and limitations
  This tests simulated-gamepad button demands after gesture recognition, through
  RaceEditor.updateRacePedalAxes and production HandlingAssist/VehicleDynamicsRunner.
  It does not emulate browser touch dispatch. Separate controller tests cover the
  double-tap/hold handbrake gesture; the slalom driver never requests handbrake.
  Candidate function injection is regression-tested against the editor path.
  This is a repeatable controller benchmark, not an expert AI driver. Failed or
  unstable cases remain in the report; completed does not mean stable or on line.
  Samples are recorded once per second; summary metrics accumulate at 60 Hz.
  Maximum case duration 90 s. Exits on >25 m path error or >1 rad roll.
  No tire forces, engine, clutch, transmission, suspension, or car files are tuned.

Saved-car caveat
  At calibration time all four named documents use AWD and 374 hp. Their brake
  bias and rev limits differ. Therefore --drivetrains=fwd,rwd adds explicitly
  synthetic drivetrain-only RTG fixtures, without modifying saved car documents.
  Always include those fixtures when claiming FWD/RWD/AWD coverage.

Comparisons and checkpointing
  Copy the two pedal modules to artifacts before editing them, then pass their
  paths with --throttle=... --brake=... for an exact controller baseline.
  --feedback=... can inject a candidate digitalPedalFeedback module.
  --resume=report.json reuses completed cases; only resume the SAME controller,
  vehicle documents, course, and surface definitions. It is not a cache validator.
  Every result is checkpointed; interrupted runs can continue without starting over.
  summarize-digital-pedals.mjs before.json after.json comparison.json reports paired
  metrics and flags regressions for review. A flag is not automatically excused
  by a faster lap. Verify stopped/completed, sideslip, path error and entry speed.
  Fixed-speed braking is the comparable stopping metric; quarter-mile exit speeds
  differ, so their subsequent stopping distances must not be compared in isolation.
  Bounded input history and no telemetry history speed up tests; exact physics
  parity against the unbounded/transient configuration has a regression test.

Selected calibration
  Throttle remains pedal-spin-travel-v10. More aggressive slowing was screened
  and rejected because gains did not carry across surfaces and drivetrains.
  Brake-tire-peak-v5 blends tire peak-slip feedback in only when the load-weighted
  supported tire peak is narrow. This preserves dry braking despite unloaded
  rear tires, while responding earlier to loose-surface lock. Original physical
  slip remains in diagnostics. Existing rear-pair protection, ABS, analog pedals,
  immediate release and the handbrake gesture remain in control.
  ABS-on results from earlier candidates may be reused where the controller
  branch is unchanged; those rows explicitly carry equivalentControllerReuse.
  A 600-step release/reapply test verifies pressure-history invariance, and the
  final report requires every ABS-on fixed-speed stopping result to match exactly.

Final report command (fails until all 480 paired cases are present)
  node tools/vehicle-validation/report-digital-pedals.mjs artifacts/digital-pedals
  Outputs summary.json and comparison.csv. Inspect all comparison regression
  flags as well as non-completions; these are measurements, not a claim that
  every car/tire/surface combination can complete a slalom at the target speed.

2026-11-19 dual throttle trial:
advanceDigitalThrottle now selects traction-focused demand for an ordinary hold,
and the unchanged advanceAggressiveDigitalThrottle for double-tap-and-hold.
The first press is immediate; the second press must start within 300ms of the
first. Release ends aggressive mode. This is pedal shaping, independent of TC;
analog/off/countdown bypass remains unchanged. Debug pedal text shows the hold
style. Unit coverage: tests/unit/digitalThrottleModes.test.js; old pedal response
regressions intentionally exercise advanceAggressiveDigitalThrottle.
Normal hold cuts sustained excess driven-wheel tread speed, with a 70ms attack /
35ms recovery feedback filter and bounded proportional demand reduction. It
stores delivered demand only; returning grip allows the existing smooth ramp.
The allowance is bounded from tire peak-slip feedback, not a grip-force change.
Live eight-second hold comparisons on asphalt/gravel use RTG AWD, BUSE FWD and

not full quarter-mile/slalom validation or proof of optimal performance.

2026-11-19 corrected-car / dual-mode calibration methodology:
The current harness explicitly drives traction holds and aggressive double-tap
holds using real button edges. Traction releases last at least 350ms; aggressive
holds start with a 50ms press, 50ms release, then held throttle. This prevents the
10Hz virtual driver accidentally changing modes while controlling slalom speed.
Each mode uses the same gesture driver for its before/after comparison.
The corrected RTG-001 AWD, CT-86 MR2 RWD, BUSE-019 Clio FWD and PZ-24 M3 RWD
retain their saved Tarmac tires and all physical specifications. These are not
matching-surface tire comparisons or a promise that every surface is passable.
There are 640 cases per complete version: 4 cars x 8 surfaces x ABS/TC on/off x
2 throttle modes x (quarter-mile and stop, 60mph stop, level/up7%/down7% slalom).
The three slaloms are separate reproducible courses, each with three left/right
cycles. Steering is fixed; only pedal response is eligible for optimization.
Default runs exercise RaceEditor wiring; candidate module runs have exact parity
coverage. Simulation uses 120Hz physics and 60Hz input, with 10Hz binary driver
decisions. Tests include failed/timed-out cases; no physical limits are bypassed.
Artifacts: artifacts/digital-pedals-dual. Baseline modules are frozen there, and
baseline-manifest.json hashes original controller, physics, car and tire inputs.
Matrix version 3 includes throttleStyle in case/resume keys; older results are
rejected. Resume only unchanged sources and fixture configuration. The dedicated
report-dual-pedals.mjs requires all 640 unique cases in both versions before it
will produce a summary and comparison CSV.

2026-11-19 17:33 UTC — v13 soft half-redline recovery trial
Normal held digital throttle now relaxes slip cuts below 50% of configured
redline, progressively through a 12%-redline band. This restores only the
existing pedal ramp; it does not force RPM, change gears or alter tire forces.
Recovery fades out over 15%-35% delivered pedal and 0.03-0.12 rad body slip.
Severe wheelspin, neutral, shifting and limiter retain protection. Release
is immediate; aggressive double-tap and analog/off bypass remain unchanged.
An initial unrestricted recovery caused CT gravel slalom failures; a pedal
gate alone still failed uphill. Both trials were rejected. The final body-slip
guard preserves all 12 level/up/down gravel slalom completions and peak beta.
94 targeted unit/integration tests pass. Final focused comparison covers
16 eight-second launches (four cars, asphalt/gravel, ABS/TC both on/off) and

RPM floor or a rerun of the full 640-case surface matrix.

2026-11-19 17:47 UTC — v14 stop compounding cuts during settling spin
Capture ad9c3565, Rally Turkey, uprated 627 hp RTG: v13 recovery was
disabled during sideways corner exits. At 10-11s, held demand fell to 8-10%
and roughly 1000 RPM. At 55-56s, RPM stayed above target while demand remained
low. Address the repeated slip cut itself, rather than raise the RPM target.
Compute fall rate from the existing filtered driven-wheel spin. Smoothly
suspend additional cuts over 0.15-0.9 spin units/s decline; fade this relief
out for excess spin 0.8-2.0. Hold current delivered demand, never increase it
purely because spin is falling. Rising/steady spin keeps normal relief.
Limiter activity or its residual resistance disables the hold. Delivered
pedal remains the stored demand; release/brake/bypass/aggressive unchanged.
95 focused tests pass, including settling vs growing slip through a slide at
30/60/120 Hz, severe spin and limiter protections, release and brake.
28 focused physical comparisons: 16 eight-second launches on asphalt/gravel
with ABS/TC on/off, 12 gravel slaloms (four original cars, aids off). All
slaloms finish; gravel launches improve modestly. Path metrics remain mixed:
BUSE uphill RMS worsens 0.69->2.39m; PZ uphill improves 3.71->2.08m but
peak yaw increases 66.3->69.6deg. No claim of universal handling improvement.
Four additional gravel comparisons use captured RTG tuning, including 627 hp,
