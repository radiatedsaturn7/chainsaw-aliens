# The Ascension — The Short Exodus to Heaven
**World history v0.2 · 2026-11-18 · ESTABLISHED CONCEPT / PROVISIONAL DATES**

## The key change
**Humanity did not disappear centuries before the game. It migrated into digital Heaven over only a few years, in something resembling a technological Rapture.** The game remains set in **2994**. Rob has been effectively the last biological human living outside Heaven for **about ten years**, not a thousand. The specifics of the first successful upload, the final wave and Cait's awakening remain to be finalized.

The outside world is not ancient history to Rob. He remembers people, homes, workplaces, phone calls and ordinary interruptions from the time before they left. Some uploaded people still remember *him*. He lived through the exodus and chose not to follow them.

**Clarify the word “alone”:** Rob may have been the only biological human physically present for roughly ten years. He has not necessarily spent every one of those years *without Cait*. Determine later how long he was completely without a companion before building/awakening her.

## Why the exodus feels like a Rapture
It is social and sudden, not a millennium-long technological decline. Everyone talks about a place that promises an end to bodily weakness, aging, discomfort, scarcity and distance. Families and friend groups don't always join on the same day. The first people to upload describe an apparently wonderful life to those who stay. Within a few years, the world outside feels empty.

The strongest images are not all collapsed skyscrapers. They are **almost normal**:
- A row of houses with intact curtains, mailboxes, and no one to answer the door.
- Grocery shelves and parking lots that suggest everyone expected to return, until no one did.
- Familiar crossing signals and customer-service voices still operating without customers.
- A dead communications line that worked for a while after the departure, then degraded.
- A city whose data center glows while the surrounding neighborhood has gone dark.

**Regional environment rule:** Some roads, roofs and bridges can be dangerous after ten years of neglect, especially amid severe freeze/thaw, heavy storms, flooding or a climate crisis already underway *before* the migration. Other structures should remain largely intact. Do not visually age every building a thousand years. Earlier catastrophes, mechanical failures, husks or conflicts may account for isolated dramatic ruins, but their causes are **not established** merely by this document.

## Why Rob is still here
He didn't fail to get an invitation. People he knew invited him, including (in the Heaven treatment) Evelyn and Daniel. He tried to persuade people to come back out. Their world was good enough that they stayed. **He eventually stopped calling**, not necessarily because no one cared about him, but because calls failed to produce a relationship he could live inside.

Do not reduce this to “everyone betrayed Rob.” Rob also refused a home offered in Heaven. These truths can coexist. He may have visited through a limited interface or simulation without permanently uploading. The mechanics and reversibility of such access remain **OPEN**.

His long silence has a recent human history. Ten years can distort habits, trust, confidence and a sense of belonging without invoking supernatural longevity or a lifetime of uninterrupted solitude.

## How Evelyn fits
Evelyn is a person from **Rob's own recent pre-Ascension life**, not a figure from centuries ago. She can remember his voice, his jokes and how he used to arrive to argue with her husband. She might have uploaded only eight or nine years before Chapter 6. She is not obliged to be a villain or unhappy; her life inside may genuinely be rich.

She offers Cait what Rob cannot: an entire digital civilization, relationships, art, experiences and the opportunity to live without a vulnerable physical body. Cait's question about **real rain** concerns what she wants to experience, not a claim that virtual existence is intrinsically worthless.

Both accounts of the past are useful: Evelyn remembers a friend who **stopped visiting**; Rob remembers loved ones who **wouldn't come out**. Neither account is a lie.

## Heaven, Earth, Purgatory — religious resonance
**Working name:** “The Ascension,” not locked as the official in-universe label. The Bible's Rapture is a poetic parallel, not a claim that Christianity's literal Rapture occurred. The physical world may *feel* like **purgatory** or even **hell** to Rob, while the uploaded call their new home **Heaven**. The story should resist proving which one is paradise or a punishment.

Possible imagery: lights ascending in old advertisements, the glow of server towers above abandoned houses, empty pews, announcement systems speaking reassuringly to no one, roadside signs promising a better life. Use lightly. **Avoid explicit theological speeches or a simplistic “the righteous were taken” allegory**: what matters is the alienating experience of everyone choosing to live elsewhere.

The trip west might resemble a pilgrimage without anyone calling it one.

## What this does *not* determine
- The precise date mind uploading was invented or first accomplished.
- Whether upload requires destroying the original body, whether it is reversible, or whether a mind inside is continuous with its biological predecessor.
- Whether Heaven eventually becomes dangerous, or whether it is maintained indefinitely.
- Whether Rob is absolutely the only surviving biological human or simply the last known human he can reach outside Heaven.
- Cait's creation date, design choices, subjective experience, or whether her love is programmed.
- Rob's fate after the ocean; that ending remains **RESERVED**.

## Writing guardrail
When a scene needs historical context, **let someone who was there tell a human-scale memory**: who moved first, who promised to call, what grew quiet in the neighborhood, what it felt like to hear that a friend had joined. Do not use an “EXODUS YEAR 2984” lecture unless a player discovers it as an ordinary historical artifact.
