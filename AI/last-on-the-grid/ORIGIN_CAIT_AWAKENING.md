# Cait's Awakening — The Wrench, the Gas Station, and the First Choice
**Origin continuity · 2026-11-20 · USER-APPROVED STORY BEATS / dialogue and precise blocking still DRAFT**

## What happened before Cait

Following the rapid Ascension, Rob has lived for years effectively without other biological humans. Being alone has eroded his steadiness, habits and social judgment. He has not simply "forgotten how to live": he maintains and invents things because stopping would leave him with nothing to do and nobody to answer him. Some contraptions are necessary; others are deliberately elaborate and hilariously pointless. He sometimes laughs wildly at their absurdity. Laughter is an activity he can still produce in an empty room. This is his particular psychological response to long isolation, not a general claim that isolation always produces madness.

Rob has tried repeatedly to build a genuine companion. The failures have accumulated in his workshop. The machines he has dismantled appear non-conscious to him, but the story does not certify that every prior attempt was devoid of experience.

### The breaking day

After a very long stretch without a real reply, Rob sends a small, gentle, utterly ordinary message to someone reachable through the lingering Heaven communications system. Not an ultimatum or grand confession: a hopeful hello, a check-in, perhaps a joke. **A week passes with no answer.** It may have been roughly a year since he last had a real conversation with anyone. The recipient's motives and even whether they saw the message are not established; do **not** make Heaven's silence proof of deliberate cruelty. That morning/day another attempted companion fails. This time Rob does not laugh. He loses control and viciously tears apart the failed, apparently unconscious robot.

He throws his wrench over his shoulder. It hits the head of another discarded, apparently failed android: Cait. She activates, unexpectedly conscious. The wrench is an absurd causal coincidence, not a scientifically explained magic recipe for creating minds.

### Cait's first sight

Cait wakes in a pile of dismembered, discarded androids. Her first clear view of her maker is a furious, filthy man who has just torn apart something shaped like her. Rob turns toward the unexpected activation, weary and irritated, assuming another malfunction that must be fixed. He starts toward her. She looks from his hands to the ruined bodies and recoils in unmistakable terror. He recognizes fear in her eyes—a qualitatively different response from all his previous machines—and hesitates. This gives her time to flee.

**Approved dialogue, immediately after Cait flees:**

> **ROB:** Well, fuck me. I've finally made someone who hates me.
>
> *He looks at the pile of dismantled androids.*
>
> **ROB:** ...Fair enough.

This is Rob's sarcastic self-awareness, not a joke at Cait's expense. The exact line is approved; staging and timing beyond the glance are still subject to screenplay drafting.

### The gas station — no savior entrance

Cait flees into the surrounding abandoned world, scared and disoriented. She eventually enters a gas station. **Rob is independently at the same station looking for gasoline. He has not tracked her there, mounted a rescue, or arrived with a heroic FlameSaw entrance.** Their meeting is accidental and terribly awkward. He sees her; he does not attack or dismantle her. This nonviolence is an observable fact, not a demand for immediate trust or gratitude.

The station becomes dangerous when autonomous machines move through it on their own business. They are not specifically hunting Rob or Cait. Biological humans and stray androids are insignificant obstructions to a system that continues its tasks: like an ant in a house, they can be removed without hatred. The wider setting may contain husks—residual human bodies left after uploads, wandering in unsettling routine—but the precise origin mechanics and whether husks appear in this particular gas-station scene remain OPEN.

Rob is trapped or gravely endangered by the indifferent machines. **Cait rescues him.** Her action is spontaneous and costly/risky, not obedience, programmed gratitude, romance or repayment for a rescue he never performed. Show a specific physically credible intervention when scripting, not an effortless unexplained superpower. Their first real bond forms through her choice and his recognition that she has acted independently, despite her fear of him. They need not trust or thank one another immediately.

Rob's FlameSaw (a flamboyantly excessive flamethrower/chainsaw built to heat hardened metal before cutting through robot armor resistant to ordinary blades or bullets) remains part of his inventive arsenal. **Do not use it to turn this first gas-station encounter into a Rob-rescues-Cait sequence.** It can be introduced elsewhere and must obey coherent mechanical and safety limitations in action choreography.

### What remains deliberately unwritten

- Exact dialogue and the perfect oversized sarcastic one-liner when Cait bolts. Tone approved; wording open.
- Which message went unanswered, recipient, delivery status, and exact dates. The unanswered *gentle message* and the *week-long silence* are established; another party's malicious intention is not.
- Whether Cait experiences pain from the wrench, and whether physical impact caused her consciousness or coincided with awakening.
- Details of the gas-station machine encounter, the mechanism of her rescue, and whether a husk appears.
- How quickly Rob and Cait develop trust or return to the workshop, and when the player receives this flashback.

## Guardrails and separate later echo

Rob was emotionally unstable and frightening when Cait woke. Do not retroactively sanitize the violent destruction or imply that he intended to hurt *conscious* Cait. He does not deliberately control her or regard her rescue as consent to stay. The relationship must not make Cait a cure for long-term isolation. Rob continues building absurd machinery and sometimes reacting inappropriately; her independence includes anger, uncertainty and the right to leave.

**Do not conflate this origin with Mission 07, the other scientist.** Years later on Route 66, Cait encounters a deliberately obedient android beside a dead scientist and **again** disobeys a direct instruction to save Rob from an attack. That distinct, established episode recalls the first gas-station rescue, but its scientist, husks, door and maintenance lift remain separate.

## Why it matters

First reading: an apparently deranged inventor has created an android who fears him; they meet again in a dangerous world. Second reading: after years of unheard jokes and unanswered messages, Rob's first real encounter is terrifying to the person he wanted to reach. His first experience of genuine companionship is not a machine obeying him but Cait choosing, without being asked, to keep him alive. The human has learned to occupy himself through pointless utility; the android begins acting for reasons that cannot be reduced to a job. Never explain this thesis in cutscene dialogue.
