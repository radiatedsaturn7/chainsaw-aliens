# MISSION 06 — HEAVEN
## Full narrative + playable mission draft v0.1

**Last on the Grid · Year 2994 · Data Center City, St. Louis, Missouri (fictional)**  
**STATUS:** PROPOSED FULL CHAPTER. New dialogue and mission mechanics are not approved canon. No game data or existing cutscene has been edited.  
**Voice:** Unhurried, capable of long thoughts, ordinary diversions, affection, irritation and contradictions. Do not perform every line as a joke or an emotional revelation.

### Basic shape
- **Arrival / setup:** The travelers need a replacement thermal-regulation module to keep their vehicle reliable on the western leg. The module exists in a self-maintaining data-center district. They are allowed in as visitors, but an automated cargo route is closed to unregistered vehicles.
- **Race:** A timed infrastructure transfer through service roads, cooling banks and flood-control corridors. No humans need rescuing; no battle to free Heaven. The player chooses between a risky short route and a slower, safer route. Success and a less-than-perfect run both allow the story to proceed.
- **Interior aftermath:** Cait meets Evelyn, an uploaded friend of Rob's, and sees a life she could choose for herself.
- **Rob / Evelyn:** A reunion shows why Rob rejected the perfected speech of people he once knew. Evelyn is still recognizable, not simply a scripted voice.
- **Departure:** Cait learns that Rob and Heaven both stopped reaching toward each other. She receives an invitation she does not accept *or* permanently reject. Their drive west resumes.
- **Narrative result:** The player understands that restoring humanity is not their campaign objective and that this “Heaven” is neither a simple prison nor an unambiguous paradise.

### PRODUCTION BOUNDARIES
- The city and race are authored ideas; do not invent “already implemented” tracks or references.
- Evelyn and Daniel are *proposed* supporting characters. Daniel is **not** established to have died or lost consciousness; his changes may be growth or emotional optimization.
- Mass uploads happened within a few years, about a decade earlier. Rob personally knew the couple before/during the migration.
- **Temporary guest access** to Heaven's simulation is a *proposed* read-only immersive visit, different from permanently transferring Cait's mind. This allows Rob's earlier recollections without deciding upload reversibility.
- “Reverse Turing Test” is an author's thematic label; **no character lectures about it**.
- Rob's future fate, Cait's final choice about Heaven, and whether her feelings equal human love remain RESERVED.

# SCENE 1 — THE LIT CITY
**EXT. OUTER SERVICE ROAD — AFTERNOON. DRIVING / IN-CAR RADIO DIALOGUE.**

Snow has blown across the deserted approach to St. Louis. Down a side street are parked cars, intact billboards, a coffee shop with cups still stacked behind the counter. A billboard reads: **WELCOME HOME. NO DISTANCE BETWEEN US.** The last word of “HOME” has lost its backlight.

Beyond the empty blocks: data-center towers, perfectly bright, ventilating plumes into the cold air. Streetlights here turn on ahead of the convoy and wink out behind it.

**CAIT:** You never mentioned there was a whole city still lit up out here. I thought we'd be scavenging through another dark warehouse. That place has enough power to run half the bloody country.

**ROB:** It probably does. Before you start getting ideas, we're not stopping to wire the garage to it.

**CAIT:** I hadn't said anything.

**ROB:** You had that look.

**CAIT:** What look? You can't even see my face. We're in different cars.

**ROB:** I can hear it.

**CAIT:** Oh, you can hear my face now? That's brilliant. We should charge people for that.

A green traffic signal waits above an empty six-lane intersection. Its pedestrian signal is counting down from twelve for nobody.

**CAIT:** Why are the roads this clean?

**ROB:** Maintenance contracts. They've got machines for everything. Snow removal, drainage, weeds, people bothering them in the middle of the night.

**CAIT:** That last one sounds useful.

**ROB:** They'd have collected you weeks ago.

Cait laughs, then goes quiet as the first tower fills the windshield.

**CAIT:** You ever been here?

**ROB:** Yeah. Years ago.

**CAIT:** Before you made me?

**ROB:** Yeah.

**CAIT:** Could've told me a bit more about it.

**ROB:** I told you the part where they've got a thermal module we need.

**CAIT:** Christ, you ought to write travel brochures.

An automated sign illuminates as they arrive:
**OUTSIDE VEHICLE DETECTED. PLEASE REGISTER FOR THE GUEST TRANSFER LANE.**

**SYSTEM VOICE:** Welcome to Heaven Infrastructure District Six. We are delighted you have arrived. Your current vehicle is not registered. Please contact your account holder to authorize delivery access.

**ROB:** There we go.

**CAIT:** You said you knew this place.

**ROB:** I do. Doesn't mean it likes me.

He attempts the old access code. The reader goes red.

**SYSTEM VOICE:** Thank you for your patience. We apologize for any inconvenience. Your comfort matters to us.

**CAIT:** I feel comforted already.

**ROB:** Wait till you hear it the fifth time.

A freight crawler crosses the lane, carrying a sealed industrial unit. Cait scans the cargo manifest on a roadside display. Its destination matches the service depot they need; the device is listed as unused emergency stock.

**CAIT:** There's one of our modules in that shipment. If we take the service loop, we can meet it at the delivery bay.

**ROB:** That road's closed to public traffic.

**CAIT:** So is every other road we've used for the last thousand miles. Look, we either run that loop or spend three days looking for a broken module in a dead shed.

**ROB:** You're starting to sound like me.

**CAIT:** Don't insult me.

A service gate begins to close behind the crawler. Rob checks the map and pauses.

**ROB:** There's a west-side entrance. Longer, but less risk of getting flattened by a delivery robot.

**CAIT:** And if the module's gone by then?

**ROB:** Then you get to tell me I was right about the scenic route.

**CAIT:** That's not how being right works.

**ROB:** Drive, Cait.

**END SCENE / PLAYER TAKES CONTROL.**

# GAMEPLAY — THE SERVICE LOOP

**Objective:** Intercept the scheduled infrastructure transfer at the staffed-by-machines delivery bay; retrieve a compatible thermal-regulation module.

**Race identity:** A fast point-to-point route through a city still running for people who no longer live physically in it. The hazards are legitimate *systems*, not enemies that inexplicably attack the protagonists.

**Stage beats (to prototype):**
1. **Empty boulevards:** clean traction with unpredictable compacted-snow patches. Tower lights and an old transit bus create scale.
2. **Freight crossing:** robotic crawlers follow schedules regardless of the player's route; flashing barriers show when a lane is about to close. Avoid collision, use deliberate timing.
3. **Cooling-bank mist:** low visibility and wet asphalt at the edge of the heat exchanger plant. Audible fan pitch gives warning before the next cloud.
4. **Two viable routes:** a direct maintenance corridor with tight gates and expensive mistake potential, or a longer drainage road with poorer surface but fewer barriers. Both rejoin at a common service plaza.
5. **Final bay:** drive into a loading zone before a departing freight transfer closes access. Do not require the player to destroy machinery or hurt uploaded minds.

**Gameplay conversation beats (triggered by position, not hard-coded times):**
- Passing a row of empty playgrounds, Cait notices the swings moving in the wind. Rob asks her to watch the road. This is not followed by a speech.
- At the first freight crawler, the system politely apologizes while almost crushing the car. Cait curses it, Rob laughs, then immediately tells her to brake.
- In the mist, Rob worries the cooling banks will ice the windshield. Cait responds that he can worry about that while she tries not to become a modern-art installation.
- Near the finish the system says, **“Thank you for choosing Heaven.”** Cait says, **“I didn't.”** Rob doesn't answer.

**Outcome design:** A clean run gets the module from the automated counter immediately. If late, a backup module is available after a short scavenging interaction; the chapter does not fail or invent a rescue mission. Dialogue references the actual performance (“That was a tidy run” versus “I thought that barrier had your name on it”). No extra plot significance for finishing first.

# SCENE 2 — SOMETHING FOR NOTHING
**INT. LOADING ATRIUM — LATE AFTERNOON.**

The module is being moved from its rack by an articulated arm. A soft-voiced automated attendant offers tea, though there is no staff to drink it.

**SYSTEM VOICE:** Would you like to take a moment to relax in our guest lounge?

**ROB:** No.

**SYSTEM VOICE:** Your comfort matters to us.

**ROB:** Yes, you said.

Cait points toward a greenhouse behind the glass, absurdly bright and alive.

**CAIT:** I'm having a look around. There's a bloody garden in there. Maybe they've got food that hasn't been frozen for ten years.

**ROB:** I'll fit the module. Don't plug yourself into anything.

**CAIT:** You know, there are nicer ways to say “have fun.”

**ROB:** Have fun. Don't plug yourself into anything.

**CAIT:** Much better.

She goes. Rob waits until she's out of earshot before looking up at a wall directory labeled **RESIDENT RECONNECTION SERVICES**. He knows the layout without consulting the map.

# SCENE 3 — EVELYN
**INT. GUEST LOUNGE / GREENHOUSE — CONTINUOUS.**

A real potted fern sits near the glass. Beyond it, projected images display a city in spring. Cait reaches for the plant, realizes there is soil, then checks the ventilation grate as though finding a trick.

A monitor lights up. Evelyn appears in a painter's apron. The brush she's holding leaves a blue line in midair where her painting is just beyond the camera.

**EVELYN:** Goodness! An actual visitor. I'm sorry, you've caught me in the middle of something. Daniel keeps telling me to turn off the reception feed when I'm painting, but I never remember. Are you looking for someone, dear?

**CAIT:** Jesus—no, I was looking for food. You nearly frightened the oil out of me.

**EVELYN:** Oh! I am sorry. I'm Evelyn. I thought the guest lounge had been closed for years. Most visitors come through the network now, and those of us inside forget there are still doors you can open.

**CAIT:** Cait. Sorry, I didn't mean to swear at you. We've been on the road since morning. What are you painting?

**EVELYN:** A canal outside my apartment in Florence. I've painted it seven times, and the little bridge still looks like a loaf of bread. I keep fixing it, then I make the water dreadful. You'd think unlimited chances would improve a person. I'm beginning to suspect they just give you more time to make a mess.

Cait leans closer to the display.

**CAIT:** That's not so bad. I mean, the bridge does look edible, but the light's nice. Is Florence still standing outside?

**EVELYN:** The real one? I don't know, I'm afraid. Ours has been lovingly rebuilt from records and everyone's recollections. It's rather nice to remember a city the way you wish it had been. I never liked the crowds, and Daniel always complained about the smell of the river. We've removed both.

**CAIT:** You've removed the smell of a river?

**EVELYN:** Well, the unpleasant sort. We keep the sea air, the flowers, the bakeries. The bread was one thing I absolutely refused to give up.

**CAIT:** You don't need to eat, though, do you?

**EVELYN:** Not a bite. But I enjoy it. People still invite one another to dinner, and we're forever trying recipes nobody could afford to make in the old days. You can have a terrible meal, which is something I sometimes do on purpose. Daniel says I'm being theatrical.

**CAIT:** So you've built an afterlife where you can ruin dinner for your husband on demand. Sounds like you've thought of everything.

Evelyn laughs more naturally than the welcome system did.

**EVELYN:** I rather like you. You've been outside all this time? Traveling alone?

**CAIT:** Not alone. I came with Rob. He needed a part for the car, and I needed somewhere not made of bloody snow. We've got another two thousand miles to go, give or take a few thousand if he gets his way with the detours.

Evelyn stills.

**EVELYN:** Rob? Robert? Tall, perpetually covered in grease, opinionated about machinery that was already obsolete when I knew him?

**CAIT:** That's him. You know him?

**EVELYN:** Oh, I should think so. We knew him before we came here. He helped Daniel put a little racing engine back together once. Daniel said he could do it without help. Rob watched him struggle for three hours, then took it apart again to show him where he'd gone wrong. They didn't speak for a week.

**CAIT:** That's the most believable thing I've heard since we arrived.

**EVELYN:** He used to visit after the uploads, too. We argued so much about the physical world. He was convinced we were missing something vital, but the longer we stayed the less we knew how to explain that we weren't unhappy. I don't think he believed us.

**CAIT:** He doesn't tell me much about it. Most of what I know is from old recordings. What was it like when everyone left?

Evelyn sets the invisible brush down. A distant chime sounds from her room.

**EVELYN:** Faster than any of us expected. For months it was an exciting novelty: someone would tell you they'd made the transfer, then you'd get a message about the lovely place they'd built. Daniel was skeptical until his sister went in. After that, everything became a rush. At my office we held a farewell lunch every Friday, then twice a week. Toward the end there was no one left to arrange the lunches.

She smiles at the absurdity, but the smile doesn't last.

**EVELYN:** The last time I went shopping in person, the cashier gave me the keys to the shop. He said the new owners wouldn't need them. Imagine that. I still have them, in here somewhere. Ridiculous thing to keep when there aren't any doors I couldn't simply open.

**CAIT:** And Rob stayed.

**EVELYN:** Oh, yes. He said there was something wrong with a world where everything could be made to suit you. I used to argue that we'd finally stopped hurting one another. He'd ask whether we were still allowed to tell each other when we'd been complete fools.

**CAIT:** Are you?

**EVELYN:** Of course. Daniel told me this morning my painting looked like a collapsed railway viaduct. I reminded him he doesn't know his arse from a paintbrush.

Cait laughs, genuinely relieved.

**CAIT:** Well, that's promising.

**EVELYN:** It wasn't a pleasant disagreement, actually. I sulked for an hour. Then he apologized, and I decided I'd rather finish painting than spend another day furious about a bridge. That's what Rob never quite understood: some of us aren't pretending to feel better. We *do* feel better.

Cait watches her, reassessing.

**CAIT:** He says it's too perfect here. Like nobody's really themselves.

**EVELYN:** He's entitled to feel that way. I don't think he ever forgave us for being happy with something he couldn't bear. But I'm not sure I can forgive him entirely for assuming our happiness was false.

A small silence. Evelyn folds her apron.

**EVELYN:** I'm sorry. I shouldn't put you in the middle of old arguments.

**CAIT:** Don't worry. I'm usually already in the middle of his arguments. What does it look like in there? I mean when you're not talking into a wall.

Evelyn brightens and turns the camera. Beyond her painting, Cait sees a sunlit balcony, people arguing over a table game, a distant tram. Someone calls to Evelyn about a concert.

**EVELYN:** You could visit. There are no registration restrictions for you—not the old biological transfer process. You're already an artificial mind, and your body could be put into a safe shutdown while you're inside. If you chose to remain, we could offer you a permanent home. Friends. Work, if you want any. Or none at all.

**CAIT:** And if I wanted to come back to this body?

**EVELYN:** We'd have to talk to the interface people. I've never done it, and I won't pretend I know the details. But you needn't decide anything today.

**CAIT:** What about racing? You've got that, surely.

**EVELYN:** Oh, we have entire competitions. One season they built circuits that spiraled around a gas giant. The racers complained about the gravity until somebody reminded them it wasn't real. You'd fit right in.

Cait considers that for longer than she expected to.

**CAIT:** What about rain? Can you feel it?

**EVELYN:** Whatever you like. I can give you a thunderstorm in this room right now if you'd prefer it.

**CAIT:** No, I mean... rain you don't get to choose. Rain that ruins your plans and soaks your boots, and doesn't care whether you're in a good mood.

**EVELYN:** You can ask for all of that.

**CAIT:** Yeah, but then I'd have asked for it, wouldn't I?

Evelyn tilts her head.

**EVELYN:** I suppose you would.

**CAIT:** I'm not saying there's anything wrong with what you've got. Honestly, it looks incredible. I'm just starting to get used to being out here. There's a whole coastline I haven't seen. The Pacific, specifically. Rob says we could go to the Atlantic, which is technically true and also an exceptionally irritating thing to say.

**EVELYN:** Sounds like him. When you come back this way, tell me whether it was worth the drive.

**CAIT:** I might. And for the record, your bridge isn't terrible. The bakery next to it looks like it's on fire, but the bridge is fine.

**EVELYN:** Oh, damn. That's supposed to be the church.

They both laugh.

**END SCENE.**

# SCENE 4 — A FRIEND WHO CHANGED
**INT. PUBLIC CONTACT BOOTH — EARLY EVENING. OPTIONAL SEQUENCE ENCOUNTER THAT SHOULD BE IN THE MAIN STORY.**

Cait returns with Rob, ostensibly to show him the greenhouse's weirdly healthy fern. He realizes who she spoke to when he sees the active terminal.

**ROB:** Evelyn.

**EVELYN:** Robert! I was hoping Cait would bring you. You look dreadful. Hasn't anyone made you eat something decent?

**ROB:** Delighted to see you too. And I eat fine, thank you.

**EVELYN:** I used to make you dinner and you'd dismantle my appliances while the potatoes cooked. Daniel still tells people about the blender.

**ROB:** It was trying to kill itself. I helped.

**EVELYN:** You broke it.

**ROB:** I improved it. It never made that noise again.

**EVELYN:** It never made *any* noise again.

Cait laughs. Rob almost does. The old rhythm is there.

**EVELYN:** Daniel would be so happy to hear your voice. I don't know why you stopped visiting. For a while he left a light on at the house, silly man. He thought you might come in one evening without telling us.

**ROB:** Yeah. I know the house. With the dock.

**EVELYN:** And the birches. He remembered how much you liked the trees. The place is still there.

**ROB:** It's a nice house. I told him that.

**EVELYN:** I know. He was pleased. We all wanted you to have somewhere comfortable.

**ROB:** I had somewhere comfortable. My own place. I wasn't exactly sleeping in a drain.

**EVELYN:** You know what I mean.

Rob scratches at a thread on his glove.

**ROB:** How's he doing?

**EVELYN:** Very well. We went through a rather difficult patch a few years back, but we learned to talk things through. He doesn't fly into those awful rages anymore.

**ROB:** He still thinks I threw my life away?

**EVELYN:** Oh, Rob. He understands now that everyone has their own path. He's learned to respect other people's choices. We all have.

**ROB:** That's not really what I asked. Last time we spoke properly, he told me I was a stubborn fool. I called him a coward. We shouted at each other until you made us leave the room. Does he still think I made a mistake?

**EVELYN:** He wouldn't put it like that. He's much more at peace with things now, and I think he would want you to be at peace, too.

Rob glances at Cait.

**ROB:** He could say he'd changed his mind. He could say he'd still disagree. I don't need him to tell me I'm doing well at being Robert.

**EVELYN:** You're right. I'm sorry. I shouldn't speak for him. I can ask if he'll come to the terminal, if you like.

It's a real offer. Rob doesn't answer immediately.

**ROB:** No. Not tonight.

**EVELYN:** Alright. But don't decide what he'd say without asking him. You used to hate it when people did that to you.

The rebuke surprises Rob. He looks almost pleased.

**ROB:** There she is.

**EVELYN:** What?

**ROB:** Nothing.

**EVELYN:** You can still come in, you know. I don't mean to stay forever. The guest service is available. We could have supper together and you could tell Daniel what you think of his ridiculous garden.

**ROB:** I tried it once. It was beautiful. Even the mosquitoes didn't bite.

**EVELYN:** Daniel was rather proud of that.

**ROB:** Yeah, well. Took the fun out of complaining about them.

**EVELYN:** I think the truth is that you never liked a world that could stop hurting you.

**ROB:** Maybe. I didn't like not knowing whether somebody meant what they said, either.

**EVELYN:** You still don't. Not even out there.

Silence. Cait takes the fern's fallen leaf and turns it over in her fingers.

**EVELYN:** I don't mean that cruelly, Rob. We weren't replaced with polite machines. Some of us really changed. Some of us were relieved to. I'm sorry you lost people you recognized. But I don't know what to do with the suggestion that we're not ourselves anymore.

**ROB:** Fair enough.

**EVELYN:** That's all? I waited years for you to say “fair enough” and it turns out to be horribly unsatisfying.

He laughs, despite himself.

**ROB:** I'll give you a proper argument next time.

**EVELYN:** You always did promise next time.

His smile fades just slightly.

**EVELYN:** Take care of each other, will you?

**CAIT:** We will. And I'll send you a photo when we reach California. If you promise not to fix the weather in it.

**EVELYN:** That sounds dreadful. I promise.

Rob nods once, and they leave. Evelyn remains on the monitor a moment longer, uncertain whether to call after him.

# SCENE 5 — THE PART THEY LEFT OUT
**EXT. DELIVERY BAY / PARKED CARS — DUSK.**

The module is installed. The city lights are reflected on the wet asphalt. Cait pulls on her gloves as Rob tightens the last fastener, even though the automated torque tool already reported the correct value.

**CAIT:** It was nice seeing you talk to someone who actually knew you. Not that I don't count, obviously, but she remembers you before all this. I didn't think I'd get to see that.

**ROB:** She always talked too much. You were lucky you escaped with your ears.

**CAIT:** I liked her. She tells proper stories. Daniel sounds like an absolute nightmare, though.

**ROB:** Daniel's alright. He'd drive you up the wall, but he could be funny. I miss the bastard.

He tests the engine. It starts cleanly.

**CAIT:** She said he kept a light on for you.

**ROB:** Yeah. He made me a house in there. Had a dock and everything. Nice trees, too. You could lie on the grass and watch the clouds go by. I went in for a couple of days.

**CAIT:** You never told me that.

**ROB:** Never came up.

**CAIT:** Rob, we spent an entire night arguing about whether you could get bored in Heaven. You might've mentioned you'd actually been.

**ROB:** You're not wrong.

Cait waits, but he doesn't add anything.

**CAIT:** Did you hate it?

**ROB:** No. That's what bothered me. It was all good. You could ask for the right weather and the right music and the right company, and there'd be no reason to get up and go anywhere else. After a while I didn't know if I was having a good time or being handed the experience of having a good time. Might be a stupid distinction.

**CAIT:** Not stupid. Maybe a bit unfair to the people who chose it, but I get what you're saying. Evelyn didn't strike me as fake.

**ROB:** I know. She never did, not entirely. Then I'd ask Daniel something and he'd give me this lovely speech about respecting my feelings. I wanted him to call me an idiot. I wanted him to tell me what he thought.

**CAIT:** Well, she did tell you off tonight. I thought you were going to hug the bloody monitor.

**ROB:** Yeah. That was nice.

Cait considers it. A soft chime from the service bay announces another automated shipment.

**CAIT:** You ever think about bringing people back outside?

**ROB:** Yeah. I tried. Spent years at it, on and off. Thought if a few people came out and had a decent meal, froze their arses off, went for a drive—maybe they'd remember something they'd missed. Trouble was, most of them didn't miss it. Why would they? They had whole lives in there.

**CAIT:** Evelyn said you stopped calling.

**ROB:** I did.

**CAIT:** Did they stop answering first?

He looks at the torque tool.

**ROB:** Not always. Sometimes they answered. Sometimes they didn't. Eventually I couldn't stand the thought of hearing somebody explain how glad they were to hear from me and then tell me they had to go to a party I'd never see. It got easier not to ask.

**CAIT:** She was pretty happy to see you tonight.

**ROB:** Yeah. She was.

There is no easy reply to that.

**CAIT:** She invited me in, too.

He places the wrench into the tool roll with more care than necessary.

**ROB:** I heard.

**CAIT:** Said I could race on tracks that go around planets. I told her I'd think about it. I'm not planning to join tomorrow, Rob. I still want to see California. I just wanted you to know I wasn't laughing it off.

**ROB:** You should think about it. It's something, having a place like that offered to you.

**CAIT:** You could try not to sound like you're telling me to go away.

**ROB:** I'm not.

**CAIT:** I know. I think.

They watch the vapor rising behind the towers.

**CAIT:** You want me to put the place on our map? In case we come back this way?

**ROB:** Yeah. Put it on. Be a shame to forget where the worst coffee in Missouri comes from.

**CAIT:** You didn't even drink their coffee.

**ROB:** Don't need to. It's perfect. Bound to be awful.

Cait smiles and writes **HEAVEN — EVELYN** on the paper map. Then, smaller, **CALL BEFORE ARRIVING**.

**CAIT:** Come on. We've got a long way to go.

Rob looks at her and closes the tool roll.

**ROB:** Yeah. We do.

The city continues glowing in the rearview mirror. It is not conquered, destroyed, or saved. Its people continue living.

**END MISSION 06.**

# LITERARY COMPANION — DEVELOPMENT NOTES

## What this chapter actually changes
**Before:** The outside world looks deserted; Rob's resistance to Heaven could be interpreted as refusal to save people who need saving. **After:** Heaven's residents may be happy, and Rob's isolation comes partly from a two-way refusal to share the same world. Cait has been offered a real alternative life and has *not* simply rejected it. Both are changed by a conversation, without a forced moral victory.

## Why these exchanges matter
| Beat | First-playthrough action | Possible second reading | Challenge to the writer |
|---|---|---|---|
| System repeats “Your comfort matters to us” | A funny, infuriating roadblock | The mechanical version of rehearsed reassurance that Rob later resents | Don't make every polite person a robot |
| Evelyn can't paint a bridge correctly | Makes her warm, creative and unoptimized | Heaven cannot make someone happy by making all effort pointless; she's chosen imperfect activity | If she is too convincingly “fake,” the ambiguity collapses |
| Evelyn remembers the last farewell lunches | Personalizes a rapid exodus | Everyone was making individual decisions that became a collective disappearance | Don't reduce a world's departure to a single villain |
| Cait wants rain she hasn't ordered | A puzzling personal preference | She values the external world as something that can surprise or resist her | Don't turn actual rain into proof digital minds are inferior |
| Daniel's lake house | A real invitation, not a lie | Rob didn't merely get abandoned; he could not live as they lived | Don't suggest accepting Heaven was the obviously correct answer |
| Evelyn challenges Rob directly | Shows she still has a sharp individual voice | Maybe Rob is wrong that all the uploads became bland and fake | The moment must surprise Rob without curing his grief |
| The unanswered calls | Rob admits he too withdrew | Love can exist alongside failed communication | Leave room for missed messages, incompatible lives, genuine affection |
| Cait writes “Call before arriving” | A practical note on a map | A small, unforced answer to how *she* intends to handle distance | Avoid treating it as a guaranteed promise she'll keep forever |

## Gameplay-literary integration
The city's automated system is useful and dangerous without malice; the race is driven by access to a needed component, not by abstract metaphysics. The player is *required* to experience perfectly courteous software treating them as a scheduling obstruction before meeting Evelyn, who uses some similarly gentle language but also surprises and disagrees. The contrast must be felt, not narrated.

## What is deliberately unsettled
1. Is Heaven's version of Daniel genuinely happier and changed, or regulated into an unrecognizable personality? **No definitive answer.**
2. Is Rob's dislike of perfection an ethical insight, an unhealthy attraction to conflict, or both? **No definitive answer.**
3. Does entering Heaven require copying, permanent transfer, or an optional guest session? **Only the proposed guest terminal is specified here; permanent mechanism OPEN.**
4. Can Cait return after entering? Evelyn does not promise it; Cait does not decide.
5. When and how the game reveals Rob's personal history with the Ascension in earlier chapters remains open.
6. The exact gameplay timings and availability of the replacement module need prototyping. The player shouldn't be punished by an unwinnable timed race.
7. Mission 07 should later challenge the comfortable ambiguity by showing an android that can say loving words by routine, while Cait herself refuses to say them lightly.

## Revision targets for review
- **A — Mechanical credibility:** The thermal module, registration gate and routing should make sense for a real racing stage, not feel like a fetch quest pasted onto the road.
- **B — Voice:** Some Evelyn speeches are longer than average. Keep them where they reveal memories or personality; cut them if they only explain Heaven.
- **C — Tension:** Decide whether a brief attempt by the system to deny Rob access (not hostile violence) would tighten the exterior scenes without casting Heaven as an evil antagonist.
- **D — The best scene may be the reunion:** Evelyn's real disagreement with Rob should be given room to land; his affection for her must be believable even though he never wanted her life.
- **E — Not a thesis defense:** No one in this chapter should say “reverse Turing test,” and no line should settle what love is.
