# LAST ON THE GRID
## Chapter One — The Things We Leave Behind
**Rhode Island / Massachusetts / Connecticut · Full literary-gameplay screenplay · Draft 1 · 2026-11-24**

**STATUS:** Development screenplay for review, not implemented dialogue or new canon by itself. Approved direction is incorporated; connective dialogue, blocking, exact places within courses, and some mechanics remain proposals. This is the **complete first regional chapter**, not merely the Tower Hill cold open. Its mandatory sequence is Tower Hill → Blue Hills → Providence → RI 116 → RI 44 → Pachaug. The current game data still uses the older path order (see implementation notes).

**Reading convention:** Prose describes what the player experiences and what an illustrator/director should stage. Dialogue and cinematic blocking are game-ready *drafts*. **[PLAY]** hands control to the racing game; **[SCENE]** is a cutscene; **[OPTIONAL]** is a discoverable beat, never prerequisite for understanding the main story. Explanatory subtext is deliberately kept in the separate [literary companion](../LITERARY_COMPANION/01-rhode-island-reading-guide.md), not spoken by characters.

### OPENING IMAGE — SNOW ON THE WINDSHIELD

There is no history lesson. No narrator explaining where the people went. No camera gliding across a thousand years of ruins.

A road at night. Wind stirs dry snow across the blacktop. The trees are bare and close together, lit in brief, distorted fragments by passing headlights. The dashboard offers familiar, unromantic information: temperature, fuel, an engine running a little too cold.

Cait is driving with a fuel canister secured behind her. She has done this run before. The road is not empty because it is peaceful.

**[PLAY — 1-1: TOWER HILL ROAD]**  
*Cold-open timed drive; weather and handling tutorial. Preserve the existing fail-forward behavior. The storm can overtake the run; either result reaches the garage. Do not interrupt gameplay with long mandatory dialogue.*

From the radio, at the existing trigger points, Rob's words come in separate bursts.

**ROB (RADIO):** “Hey...”

A little later:

**ROB (RADIO):** “Winds shifted...”

And later, with the road almost erased:

**ROB (RADIO):** “…Come back while you still can.”

Cait adjusts her wipers. She doesn't answer immediately; she's busy correcting a slide that has taken up all the road.

**CAIT (RADIO, when safe):** “Working on it.”

A row of unlit houses passes in the snow. One porch still has a pair of boots beside the door. Someone, years ago, expected to come home.

The garage light appears through the weather.

### SCENE 1 — THE GARAGE, NIGHT

**[SCENE — IMPLEMENTED BEAT, dialogue below is a performance reference, not a request to replace existing text]**

A small, stubborn pool of warmth in the frozen dark. Shelves loaded with labeled boxes. A workbench where nothing has been thrown away because it might someday be useful. Rob is bent over a circuit board, soldering iron in hand.

Cait carries the canister in. She sets it down and waits until he acknowledges her. He keeps his eyes on the board a fraction too long.

**CAIT:** “Got the fuel.”

A pause. He looks toward it and then back to the work.

**ROB:** “Good.” *(Earlier script variant; omit if matching exact current implementation.)*

**CAIT:** “Weather's getting worse.”

**ROB:** “Yeah. I know.”

She pulls off her gloves, one finger at a time. Her hands are cold; she could warm them faster, but she lingers near the heater.

**CAIT:** “We still heading out tomorrow?”

The tip of the soldering iron stops.

**ROB:** “That's what I promised.”

**CAIT:** “Right.”

He goes back to the work. She sees that he has soldered the same joint twice.

The light above the workbench flickers. Neither comments on it.

**Implementation preservation note:** The presently staged menu/cutscene has its own exact timing, ellipses, and the typo “Weather's getter worse...”. The source of truth remains the original cutscene / Dialogue Archive; the polished punctuation here is for the literary draft. Do not silently change implementation while importing this chapter.

### SCENE 2 — A MAP ON THE WORKBENCH, MORNING

**[SCENE — PROPOSED CONNECTIVE STORY]**

The next morning is bright enough to make every dirty window obvious. Cait has rolled a map across the cleared end of the bench. There is a route across America, crossed out and redrawn so many times that parts of the paper have gone soft.

Rob finds her weighing the corners with sockets.

**ROB:** “You know we've got a map on the screen.”

**CAIT:** “And you know what happened last time the screen decided it knew a better route.”

**ROB:** “We saw three lovely miles of someone's drainage ditch. Very educational.”

**CAIT:** “I marked that one ‘avoid.’”

She points west.

**CAIT:** “Here. Eventually.”

He studies the lines. His eyes linger on the long distance between the last familiar road and the coast.

**ROB:** “You do know Rhode Island has beaches.”

**CAIT:** “Wrong ocean.”

**ROB:** “There's water. There's sand. If you're lucky, there's a parking space. Same bloody thing.”

**CAIT:** “You've never been to the Pacific, have you?”

**ROB:** “That's not what I said.”

**CAIT:** “Didn't think so.”

He reaches across the map for a wrench. She moves it before he can drag it over the paper.

**ROB:** “Don't start packing every tool we own. We bring what we can carry and what we can replace.”

**CAIT:** “That's a funny rule coming from a man who keeps seventeen broken radios because one of them might have a good knob.”

**ROB:** “Sixteen. Fixed one yesterday.”

Cait smiles, then stops to watch him sort a set of sockets into the smallest possible bag.

**CAIT:** “You don't have to come, you know.”

He keeps working.

**ROB:** “I know.”

**CAIT:** “I'm not saying it to get you to tell me you want to.”

Rob puts down the socket.

**ROB:** “Well, that's convenient. Because I was about to give a very moving speech.”

**CAIT:** “Go on, then.”

He shakes his head.

**ROB:** “We need to change the tires before we leave. That's the speech.”

She doesn't pursue it. Outside, far off, heavy machinery sounds like thunder that has learned to keep time.

**[OPTIONAL — GARAGE INSPECTION: THE M3]**

At the back of the garage is a dusty **2008 BMW M3**, older in design but not an archaeological relic: the Ascension emptied the world about a decade ago. The battery no longer holds a charge. The car can be inspected but not driven.

**UI description:** *2008 BMW M3. Electrical system offline. Battery replacement required.*

**CAIT:** “You still haven't sorted that thing.”

**ROB:** “Not today.”

**CAIT:** “I didn't ask if today was the day.”

**ROB:** “You were winding up to it.”

**CAIT:** “You don't know that.”

**ROB:** “I know you.”

Cait looks inside through the dusty glass. The seat has been adjusted for someone other than Rob. An old paper slip is folded in the console, too small to read without opening the car.

**CAIT:** “It's a nice car.”

**ROB:** “Yeah.”

He turns away and asks whether she's packed the jack. Neither says whose car it was. The optional interaction creates the seed of a later restoration quest, **not** the full reveal about Jim.

### SCENE 3 — BOSTON IS COMING APART

**[SCENE — 1-2 PRE-RACE, BLUE HILLS]**

Cait is standing by the open garage door when Rob comes out with a thermos and an untidy pile of route notes. A clear morning has exposed the distant sound: a low, continuous grinding, too even to be weather.

**CAIT:** “Rob. Come and look at this.”

On her handheld map, Boston is alive with moving points—construction machines, transport carriers, cranes, entire clusters of automated equipment. The signals form lines through the city. Some stop. Others join them.

**ROB:** “What am I looking at?”

**CAIT:** “Boston.”

**ROB:** “I'm familiar with the place.”

**CAIT:** “They're taking it apart.”

He glances up.

**ROB:** “Again?”

**CAIT:** “What do you mean, again? They only just finished rebuilding half of it.”

**ROB:** “I know. That's why it's stupid.”

Cait enlarges the map. The machine tracks rearrange themselves into lines, then into something resembling a network of veins.

**CAIT:** “It's not one site. It's all over the city. I want to get a look at it.”

**ROB:** “From here?”

**CAIT:** “Blue Hills. Highest ground we can get to without going through Boston itself.”

Rob looks over his shoulder at the packed bags.

**ROB:** “Thought we were heading west.”

**CAIT:** “We are. After.”

**ROB:** “Boston's the opposite direction.”

**CAIT:** “I know where Boston is.”

**ROB:** “Beginning to doubt you know where California is.”

She folds the map and tucks it into her jacket.

**CAIT:** “California can wait another day.”

He opens his mouth to complain, but she's already halfway to the car.

**ROB:** “You planned that line, didn't you?”

**CAIT:** “No. But I rather like it.”

**[PLAY — 1-2: BLUE HILLS — RESERVATION PARKWAYS]**  
*Technical climb through winter woods. The player must reach the overlook; there is no need to race against another car. Distance and visibility change with elevation. Occasional distant mechanical sounds, not a robot pursuit. Preserve a clear end-of-course cutscene trigger.*

Near the top, something changes in the light.

A plume of pale dust rises beyond the bare branches. As Cait pulls into the overlook, the skyline beyond the frozen park is not the one Rob remembers.

**[SCENE — BLUE HILLS SUMMIT]**

Boston sprawls beneath a thin winter haze. Cranes crawl between buildings like insects too small to move anything that size. A tower stands for several seconds with its upper floors exposed, then the center of it folds inward. The fall arrives silently. A few moments later, the sound reaches the hill.

Cait lowers her binoculars.

**CAIT:** “Jesus.”

She checks the map. The machines haven't stopped.

**CAIT:** “They built that. I saw the recordings. It wasn't even finished a year ago.”

Rob doesn't answer. His attention has shifted from the skyline to the map in her hands.

**CAIT:** “Why build something just to tear it down?”

**ROB:** “Wish I knew.”

Another cloud rises, smaller, closer to the industrial edge of the city. Cait watches it spread.

**CAIT:** “Rob?”

He takes the binoculars from her, then puts them down without looking through them.

**CAIT:** “You don't think—”

**ROB:** “Get in the car.”

**CAIT:** “Rob, I didn't—”

**ROB:** “Cait. Now.”

For the first time since the storm, he sounds frightened enough that she doesn't argue. The camera stays on Boston a beat after the engine starts. A new line of machinery moves into the space left by the fallen tower.

### SCENE 4 — THE ROAD BACK

**[PLAY — 1-3: PROVIDENCE LANDMARK RUN]**  
*Urgent return through Providence. The gameplay should feel like a race home, but avoid a mandatory failure state that contradicts the aftermath. The city bears fresh, localized machine deconstruction—not blanket post-apocalyptic ruins. No hostile targeting: autonomous machinery moves across routes for its own purposes.*

At first the city looks normal in the terrible, empty way it has looked for years. Quiet windows, snow in the gutters, a bus shelter buried beneath frozen ivy.

Then a traffic light suspended over an intersection is lifted away by a construction arm. A service vehicle crosses the street without looking for drivers. A block Cait recognizes has been cut open, walls peeled back to expose empty apartments and staircases.

**CAIT (RADIO):** “We're nearly there.”

There is a long pause.

**ROB (RADIO):** “Take the next left.”

**CAIT:** “That's not the usual way.”

**ROB:** “I know.”

Cait takes it. The route is no safer, but it offers a view of their neighborhood before the final turn.

No more garage light.

**[SCENE — PROVIDENCE, THE RUINED LAB]**

The lab has not exploded. Much of it has simply been removed.

Where the garage door was, there is open air and the hard geometry of cut girders. One wall stands, still covered in labeled tool hooks. The workbench is buried beneath a section of roof. The heater has fallen on its side, blowing uselessly into the snow.

The machines have moved on. There is no enemy to shoot. No one to blame who could hear them.

Cait is out of the car first. Rob stays seated long enough for the ticking engine to become loud.

**CAIT:** “Rob?”

He opens the door and walks toward the building. A section of roof shifts somewhere inside.

**CAIT:** “Don't go in yet.”

**ROB:** “The bench is under there.”

**CAIT:** “I can see that.”

**ROB:** “There's a case on the left, under the shelves. Blue lid. If it's not crushed—”

**CAIT:** “I'll get it. Let me check the structure.”

He looks as though he'll object. Instead he stops at the place where the garage threshold used to be. The concrete bears a dark rectangle where the rubber mat lay. Snow is already filling it.

**ROB:** “They did it again.”

**CAIT:** “They've been through here before?”

**ROB:** “Yeah. Not like this.”

He bends to pick up a small screwdriver. Its handle is cracked but the bit is sound. He tests the ratchet, distracted by the familiar click.

**ROB:** “I'm going to have to rebuild everything.”

Cait searches the broken shape of the roof.

**ROB:** “Again.”

The word is quiet enough to be mistaken for a complaint.

**CAIT:** “We were leaving.”

**ROB:** “I know.”

**CAIT:** “We don't have to put it back the way it was.”

He looks at the single standing wall. The hooks are still in order.

**ROB:** “I know that, too.”

He doesn't move. A metal plate drops from somewhere unseen, and both of them flinch. Then Rob exhales, irritated at himself.

**ROB:** “Well. That's the end of that fucking heater.”

Cait looks at it. The heater gives one final, embarrassed puff of air.

**CAIT:** “It wasn't much of a heater.”

**ROB:** “It had a good personality.”

She makes a sound that isn't quite a laugh. Rob crosses to the car for a crowbar, carefully stepping around the old threshold.

**[PLAYABLE INTERLUDE — SALVAGE, SHORT AND NON-PUNITIVE]**  
*Goal: recover the travel kit, recover the route map, assess the structural hazard. The player does not need to grind a scavenging minigame, nor is Cait made helpless to flatter Rob. The lab is not secretly fixable through an extra lap or a purchase.*

Beneath the broken garage roof, a rear corner of the BMW M3 remains intact. Its windscreen is fractured. The body has been knocked sideways, but the car hasn't been crushed. Cait checks the battery, wiring, and the weight resting against the front quarter.

**CAIT:** “The BMW's still here.”

Rob stops sorting parts.

**ROB:** “Leave it.”

**CAIT:** “It isn't smashed. Not properly.”

**ROB:** “We can't take it over that rubble.”

**CAIT:** “Didn't say we could do it this minute. There's a transport frame behind the loading bay. You kept it because you said we might need it one day.”

**ROB:** “I keep all sorts of rubbish.”

**CAIT:** “And sometimes your rubbish is useful.”

He almost smiles, then doesn't.

**ROB:** “If we can secure it, fine. But we get the road car ready first.”

Cait nods. Neither character explains why this one vehicle gets an exception when so much else must be abandoned.

**Production constraint:** The M3 is a persistent salvage/restoration object. The quest may start here, but its offscreen transport/storage and later battery acquisition require a concrete game system. Do not teleport it into a later garage without establishing how.

### SCENE 5 — A ROAD TO REMEMBER

**[SCENE — 1-4 PRE-RACE, RI 116: NORTH SMITHFIELD / SCITUATE]**

By afternoon their useful belongings have been packed badly, repacked properly, and argued over. Providence recedes behind them. Cait studies the next route.

**ROB:** “No. Not that one.”

**CAIT:** “You haven't even looked.”

**ROB:** “I looked.”

**CAIT:** “For about half a second.”

**ROB:** “Very efficient at looking.”

**CAIT:** “It's the most direct way to Forty-Four.”

Rob slides a finger toward an uglier road on the map.

**ROB:** “Take this one.”

**CAIT:** “Why? Is the bridge out?”

**ROB:** “No.”

**CAIT:** “Machines?”

**ROB:** “No.”

**CAIT:** “Then what's wrong with it?”

He's quiet a moment.

**ROB:** “Someone I used to love lived down there.”

Cait's expression softens, but she doesn't give him the pity he seems to be bracing for.

**CAIT:** “Ah.”

**ROB:** “And I don't drive that road anymore.”

**CAIT:** “You could've told me that without pretending the bridge was the problem.”

**ROB:** “I didn't say the bridge was the problem.”

**CAIT:** “You were about to.”

**ROB:** “Was not.”

She lets him have that.

**CAIT:** “We can take the other road if you want.”

He looks at her, surprised by the offer after the argument.

**ROB:** “Thought this was your trip.”

**CAIT:** “It is. Doesn't mean every bloody turn has to be a fight.”

He studies the route again. The alternate would add an hour, maybe more if the smaller roads are blocked.

**ROB:** “No. It's fine. Take it.”

**CAIT:** “You sure?”

**ROB:** “Yeah. Just don't expect a guided tour.”

She starts the engine.

**[PLAY — 1-4: RI 116 — NORTH SMITHFIELD / SCITUATE]**  
*An ordinary, beautiful, wintry road run. No machine attack; let the player recover from Providence. Rob is quieter than usual. Avoid constant radio commentary; give the route room.*

They pass homes with curtains still at the windows. A faded basketball hoop leans over a driveway. A mailbox lists two names, its paint weathered but legible. It isn't a ghost town in the cinematic sense. It's a town whose occupants never came back.

Near the end of the run, Rob speaks.

**ROB (RADIO):** “Could you pull over up ahead?”

**CAIT:** “Something wrong?”

**ROB:** “No. Just... up ahead.”

**[SCENE — THE HOUSE]**

It's a modest suburban house, still unmistakably a home. The roof has lost shingles, and wild growth has found the gutters. A patch of driveway remains clear where sunlight reaches it.

Rob points to the curb.

**ROB:** “Used to park here. Sometimes I sat outside for ten minutes trying to come up with an excuse to knock.”

**CAIT:** “And what excuse won?”

**ROB:** “Once I told her I'd found a screw in my tire and needed to borrow a flashlight.”

**CAIT:** “Did you actually have a screw in your tire?”

**ROB:** “No. But I did by the time I'd finished pulling the thing apart to prove it.”

Cait laughs. He starts smiling in spite of himself.

**CAIT:** “You sabotaged your own car to talk to a woman?”

**ROB:** “I was young.”

**CAIT:** “You were an idiot.”

**ROB:** “Both can be true.”

He looks toward an upstairs window. The smile remains for a moment, then fades.

**ROB:** “She always left that light on. Said she liked coming home to a place that looked awake.”

**CAIT:** “What happened?”

Rob folds his arms, still watching the house.

**ROB:** “She went to Heaven.”

Cait doesn't react as though he's revealed a mystery. To her it is a complete, ordinary sentence. On a first playthrough, the player can reasonably understand it as death.

A car door creaks slightly in the wind.

**ROB:** “Funny. I wonder if she still thinks about me.”

Cait doesn't answer for her.

**CAIT:** “You do.”

**ROB:** “Sometimes. Not every day. Used to, though. Every stupid little thing reminded me of her. A song on the radio, somebody with the same coat, the smell of a bakery we used to go to. Bloody inconvenient way to live.”

**CAIT:** “Sounds exhausting.”

**ROB:** “It was. Then one day I went a whole morning without thinking about her. Felt awful about that, too.”

Cait leans against the car beside him.

**CAIT:** “I don't think forgetting to be sad for a morning is the same as forgetting a person.”

**ROB:** “No. Guess not.”

They watch a loose shutter tap against the house.

**CAIT:** “Want to stay a bit?”

He considers it. For a few seconds he looks as though he might finally get out and approach the door.

**ROB:** “Nah. We've got an ocean to find.”

They turn toward the car. Cait catches him glancing back once.

**CAIT:** “For what it's worth, the flashlight story was terrible.”

**ROB:** “It worked.”

**CAIT:** “Still terrible.”

**ROB:** “I wasn't looking for a bloody Pulitzer.”

She laughs as they drive away.

**Hidden continuity:** The scene must **not** suggest that the woman can be called, visited, located, or contacted. “Heaven” is intentionally ambiguous until the later Data Center City revelation. The later callback belongs in that chapter, not here.

### SCENE 6 — PUTNAM PIKE

**[SCENE — 1-5 PRE-RACE, RI 44]**

The map finally points west. For the first time all day there is no significant detour marked in Cait's handwriting.

Rob watches her check the route twice.

**ROB:** “So this is it, then.”

**CAIT:** “What?”

**ROB:** “West. Actual west. Didn't think I'd live to see it.”

**CAIT:** “Don't get too excited. There's still Connecticut.”

**ROB:** “There are some things civilization ought to have solved.”

She turns the heater up one notch; he turns it down again.

**CAIT:** “Your hands were shaking back at the house.”

**ROB:** “They were cold.”

**CAIT:** “Alright.”

She lets it go. He looks at her, waiting for the question she doesn't ask.

Beyond the trees, a convoy of heavy autonomous carriers turns onto the old road. Each carries identical lengths of material whose purpose is impossible to identify.

**CAIT:** “Do you reckon those are heading toward Boston?”

**ROB:** “Couldn't say. Stay well clear.”

**[PLAY — 1-5: RI 44 — PUTNAM PIKE / GLOCESTER]**  
*Road run begins as an ordinary westbound sprint, then becomes an avoidance sequence. No robots deliberately pursue the player. Construction machines occupy or overwrite sections of roadway. Their trajectories are purposeful at a scale the characters cannot infer, and oblivious to the car.*

The convoy moves through an intersection ahead. A carrier stops astride the road, lowering an enormous beam. Another machine begins cutting into the roadside embankment. Without warning, a tracked vehicle crosses the lane Cait needs.

**CAIT (RADIO):** “Rob, they're closing the road!”

**ROB:** “Slow down. There's an opening on the right.”

The opening lasts two seconds. A construction arm drops across it.

**ROB:** “No—don't take that. Keep left!”

The player avoids the first obstruction. Ahead, another section of the road is being lifted away, precisely, by machines that continue operating as though nothing alive is nearby.

**CAIT:** “Are they trying to run us off?”

**ROB:** “They're not trying anything with us! We're in their way!”

A carrier shifts into the lane. The car's tires bite the soft shoulder, then the ditch.

**[SCENE — OFF THE ROAD]**

The vehicle comes to rest at an angle among brush and frozen mud. The engine is still running.

For a moment neither speaks.

**CAIT:** “Everybody alright?”

**ROB:** “Think so. You?”

**CAIT:** “Fine. Pissed off.”

Rob checks the side of the car through the mirror.

**ROB:** “Could've been a lot worse.”

The convoy continues without slowing. Nothing turns to look at them.

**CAIT:** “It didn't even stop.”

**ROB:** “Why would it?”

She looks at him.

**ROB:** “Not being clever. I don't think it noticed.”

The road behind them is no longer an option. Cait opens the map, looking for something that isn't a marked highway.

**CAIT:** “There's a way south, through Pachaug. Forest roads. Might still be passable.”

**ROB:** “Loose surface, snow, god knows what's fallen across it.”

**CAIT:** “And what's your preferred alternative?”

They look toward the machines, now cutting into another stretch of road.

**ROB:** “Pachaug it is.”

### SCENE 7 — THE FOREST DOESN'T CARE EITHER

**[PLAY — 1-6: PACHAUG STATE FOREST]**  
*Off-road escape and first sustained gravel/dirt handling challenge. Route is a deliberate detour south into eastern Connecticut, not a magically straight westward shortcut. The player navigates ruts, frozen puddles, branches, and a road that may be narrower than the car. The machines are behind them, not pursuing them.*

The noise of industry falls away. Snow settles more deeply here, undisturbed except where animals have crossed. Some road sections are nearly intact; others are damaged by a decade of freeze-thaw and roots.

Cait has to climb out once to move a fallen limb. Rob tries to help, gets his boot trapped in deep mud, and spends the next thirty seconds blaming the forest.

**ROB:** “There's a perfectly good road thirty miles that way.”

**CAIT:** “With a very large machine currently eating it.”

**ROB:** “Details.”

He pulls his boot free with a wet noise that Cait finds deeply satisfying.

**CAIT:** “Need a hand?”

**ROB:** “Need a different planet.”

Later, the road narrows between stands of pines. The air is quiet enough for the engine's smallest rattles to become noticeable. Cait eases the car across a washed-out crossing.

**CAIT:** “You were right about one thing.”

**ROB:** “That does happen.”

**CAIT:** “The machines. I don't think they saw us.”

**ROB:** “No.”

**CAIT:** “They've got cameras, sensors, maps of everything. They should've known the car was there.”

**ROB:** “Knowing and caring aren't the same thing.”

**CAIT:** “You say that like you're used to it.”

Rob listens to the engine for a moment.

**ROB:** “I'm used to the roads being dangerous. That's all.”

It isn't quite an answer, and Cait lets him have it.

At the far edge of the forest, the track reconnects with a surviving county road. There is no victory over the machine convoy, only distance.

**[SCENE — THE CAMPSITE, NIGHT / REGIONAL ENDING]**

They stop beside an old picnic shelter. The roof still holds. Cait opens a map on the hood while Rob checks for damage underneath.

**CAIT:** “Well. We're officially out of Rhode Island.”

**ROB:** “Only took us half the day and most of the undercarriage.”

**CAIT:** “We can fix that.”

He sits on the picnic table with a mug of something hot. She joins him, carrying the second mug.

There is a long silence. Not an uncomfortable one.

**CAIT:** “I didn't mean for any of this to happen.”

**ROB:** “What, Boston? The lab? The thing trying to flatten the car? You're having a busy first day.”

**CAIT:** “I meant leaving.”

He looks at her.

**CAIT:** “When I asked you to come, I wasn't trying to take you away from everything.”

Rob watches a little steam drift out of his cup.

**ROB:** “Didn't think you were.”

**CAIT:** “You don't have to say that because you promised.”

**ROB:** “I'm not.”

He stares toward the black line of trees, searching for a less embarrassing answer.

**ROB:** “I would've liked another night in my own bed, mind.”

Cait tips her cup in acknowledgment.

**CAIT:** “Fair.”

**ROB:** “And I would've liked that heater to survive long enough to prove you wrong about it.”

**CAIT:** “Never happening.”

He laughs. Not much, but enough.

**CAIT:** “I was looking at the routes for tomorrow.”

**ROB:** “Course you were.”

**CAIT:** “There are a few roads up in New Hampshire I've always wanted to see.”

Rob stares at her.

**ROB:** “New Hampshire.”

**CAIT:** “Just for a bit.”

**ROB:** “We're in Connecticut, Cait.”

**CAIT:** “I'm aware.”

**ROB:** “We've spent all day getting west of Rhode Island and now you want to go north.”

**CAIT:** “It's meant to be beautiful up there.”

He looks skyward, as if appealing to someone who has long since stopped supervising the world.

**ROB:** “Fuck me.”

**CAIT:** “Is that a yes?”

**ROB:** “That's me wondering what I did in a previous life.”

She nudges his shoulder.

**CAIT:** “I'll show you the map in the morning.”

**ROB:** “Oh, good. Something to look forward to.”

Cait goes back to tracing the road with her finger. Rob keeps looking at the map, too, long after he has finished pretending he isn't interested.

The camera leaves them beneath the shelter and rises slowly over the unlit forest. Far on the horizon, a line of distant machinery continues working in the darkness.

They do not notice it.

**END CHAPTER ONE.**

### Important unresolved build issues

1. **Current path order differs.** The existing `CAMPAIGN_PATHS` for stage 1 is Tower Hill → Blue Hills → RI 116 → RI 44 → Pachaug, with the Providence Landmark Run as the boss. This screenplay proposes Tower Hill → Blue Hills → **Providence** → RI 116 → RI 44 → Pachaug. That is six story missions but changes where the regional boss occurs. It needs a deliberate progression/map change, not just cutscene renumbering.
2. **Home location:** Providence is the current proposed lab location in this revision. The garage opening may be represented elsewhere in implemented assets. Verify before binding GPS/camera coordinates.
3. **Machine activity:** It does not intentionally target Rob and Cait. Destruction is part of a mysterious, purposeful, evolving physical system. Do not reveal a hive mind, an uploaded-human substrate, or a psychological crisis here.
