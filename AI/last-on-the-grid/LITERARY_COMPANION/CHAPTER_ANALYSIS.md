# Author's Companion — Chapter-by-Chapter Literary Analysis
**v0.1 · Development tool, not required player reading**

**Purpose:** For each mission, identify its enacted conflict, its possible second meaning, a legitimate counterreading and the evidence the eventual *script* must actually contain. If a second meaning exists only on this page and never on screen, it is not earned.

## 01 — Come Back
**Literal scene:** Fuel-return drive through snow; a worried radio call; a spare garage exchange. **Subtext:** Rob can show concern without giving orders; Cait can be competent and still have someone worrying about her. **Competing reading:** Rob's concern might be fear of isolation as much as love. **Earn it:** He does not demand she obey him. The next-morning map reveals he is accompanying a destination Cait chose. **Failure test:** A narrator need not tell us he is lonely.

## 02 — Pointless Detour
**Literal scene:** Climb a perilous mountain for an automated distress repeater. **Subtext:** Cait seeks a place she expects to be empty; Rob knows what emptiness costs. **Competing reading:** Her curiosity might be selfish when fuel and safety matter. **Earn it:** Make the climb physically costly, and let her answer for having insisted on it. **Failure test:** Do not teach the player the moral in the summit conversation.

## 03 — A Stray (optional)
**Literal scene:** Rob leaves food for a stray dog. **Subtext:** Rob can care without demanding a return, even if he cannot accept care that way himself. **Competing reading:** The gesture may be impractical and endanger their supplies. **Earn it:** Show the real resource tradeoff, and let Cait be reasonably concerned. **Failure test:** The dog is not a metaphor spelled out by either character, and the scene can be cut without collapsing the overall plot.

## 04 — No Answer
**Literal scene:** A blocked Ohio River crossing and a functioning relay that cannot talk back. **Subtext:** A man who solves technical problems cannot repair an unanswered relationship. **Competing reading:** His stubborn technical curiosity might actually be useful, not only desperate. **Earn it:** The relay must give them valuable road data even though it gives him no human voice. **Failure test:** Distinguish this from Chapter 02's distress-signal mystery.

## 05 — Business as Usual
**Literal scene:** Trucks and machines still making deliveries and consumer goods. **Subtext:** Service can persist after the meaning of service is gone. **Competing reading:** Ordinary machines doing jobs are not automatically tragic; maybe the work has new uses. **Earn it:** Include a functional obstacle and somebody/something genuinely benefiting from the system, alongside the absurd waste. **Failure test:** Do not make Cait sound like an ethics lecturer.

## 06 — Heaven
**Literal scene:** An uploaded woman invites Cait to abandon her body and join a perfected virtual society. **Subtext:** The comfort of a perfect world might be real; so is the grief of those outside it. **Competing readings:** “Rob was abandoned” versus “Rob refused the invitation.” **Earn it:** Evelyn must be warmly credible in a longer conversation, and Rob's own recollection must be true but partial. **Failure test:** Without Rob's personal history, Heaven is a generic sci-fi lore dump. **World issue:** Year 2994 and Rob's history with Evelyn need an explanation.

## 07 — The Other Scientist
**Literal scene:** A service android mechanically says loving words; a husk attack; Cait disobeys and saves Rob. **Subtext:** Forced words and chosen actions cannot be equated. **Competing reading:** Cait's ability to choose does not automatically prove her attachment has no designed influences. **Earn it:** She disobeys in a believable emergency; the android's routine is disturbing without indulgent spectacle. **Failure test:** Do not conclude “therefore Cait definitely loves Rob” in narration or dialogue.

## 08 — Somewhere to Belong
**Literal scene:** Cait enters a friendly competition, makes peers and is invited to stay; skilled local technicians render Rob's repairs unnecessary. **Subtext:** They can want each other when they do not need one another. **Competing reading:** Rob's quiet hurt may be unfair to Cait, who has finally found joyful company. **Earn it:** Let the settlement have life beyond testing the couple, and include at least one genuine happy scene. **Failure test:** Cait must invite Rob along; he should have the option of joining her.

## 09 — The Cost
**Literal scene:** Cait gives scarce supplies to someone at risk; the choice damages their own options. **Subtext:** Agency includes consequences, and care between two people is not the absence of disagreement. **Competing reading:** Cait's act can be morally decent and still inconsiderate toward Rob. **Earn it:** Rob proposes a plausible alternative and the stranger has a specific need. **Failure test:** The game mustn't make players suffer arbitrarily for an authored decision.

## 10 — Still Here
**Literal scene:** Cait repairs the car, provides care and refuses the words Rob wants; canyon radio trouble makes him think she has left. **Subtext:** Rob **sees** her care and still needs a declaration. Cait **chooses** him and still cannot make that declaration honestly. **Competing readings:** Words matter; pressure can poison words. Both are true. **Earn it:** Full-length, uneven speech; genuine irritation; after the argument, Cait is doing a practical road-scouting task, not punishing him with absence. **Failure test:** “She returned” must not be presented as a cure for his insecurity.

## 11 — Wrong Ocean
**Literal scene:** Final mountain race, then arrival at the actual Pacific. **Subtext:** The same road was Cait's chosen experience and Rob's promise; both have gained and feared things along it. **Competing reading:** Perhaps the journey was as much an avoidance mechanism for Rob as it was a gift. **Earn it:** Bring back specific mundane items and running jokes rather than imposing a philosophical verdict. **Failure test:** Do not quietly make the reserved post-ocean ending canon.

## Four whole-story debates worth earning
1. **Is Rob unreliable or simply incomplete?** Evidence: what Evelyn remembers, what Rob remembers, and what we actually witness.
2. **Does Cait's designed origin diminish her attachment?** Evidence: the obedient android, Cait's own choices, and the absence of an easy engineering proof.
3. **Is Rob wrong to want the words?** Evidence: his history of rejection *and* the way his demands hurt Cait.
4. **Is Heaven a failure of human connection, or a valid place that Rob couldn't accept?** Evidence: believable lives inside it, real neglect outside it, and nobody made into a straw villain.

## Teacher's-guide anti-cheat rule
A literary interpretation is a **hypothesis with textual evidence**, not an instruction to the audience. For each claim above, a final reviewer should be able to cite a line, gesture, game mechanic or consequence in the completed script. If they cannot, we must improve the script or remove the interpretation.
