# Last on the Grid — Narrative Constitution
**Working rules · v0.1 · 2026-11-17 · REVIEW, not approved canon**

## The story we're telling
In 2994, about ten years after most humans rapidly migrated into a digital “Heaven,” a remaining biological human and an android drive from frozen Rhode Island to the Pacific because **Cait has chosen to see that ocean** and Rob has promised to take her. This is not a quest to save civilization. It is a road story about what it means to love, be understood, and choose someone when neither usefulness nor certainty can guarantee affection.

**Dramatic question:** Can Rob and Cait recognize a real relationship when they cannot agree on what to call it?
**Rob's emerging mistaken equation:** useful → needed → kept → loved. His *pre-Cait* inventions primarily stave off maddening isolation; later, once someone can leave him, usefulness becomes one way he tries to earn and verify affection. He wants freely chosen love, never control.
**Cait's uncertainty:** she cares, chooses, resists, jokes, loves racing, and is not defined by Rob. But being designed for companionship makes her wonder whether her attachment is a choice she can trust.
**The recurring conflict:** Rob needs to hear the words “I love you.” Cait demonstrates affection in actions but will not say the words if she cannot vouch for what they mean to her. Words matter to Rob; accuracy and autonomy matter to Cait. Neither should be reduced to the villain or to a correct answer.

## Approved origin and characterization constraint (November 20)
[Cait's Awakening](ORIGIN_CAIT_AWAKENING.md) is controlling for the origin. Alone for years, Rob invents both for survival and for absurd amusement; doing things that make no sense keeps the silence from swallowing the day. The failed companion / unanswered gentle message (a week without reply) / violent dismantling / wrench awakening / Cait's terror and flight are intentional. Rob and Cait independently meet again at a gas station; he does **not** rescue her there, and she saves him from autonomous machines that regard people as disposable obstructions, not targets of hatred. Do not portray Rob's building as only an attempt to earn Cait's affection or portray his need for love as a wish to own/control her. Preserve the later Mission 07 separate rescue. Let painful ambiguity and sarcasm coexist without making Cait's fear funny.

## Non-negotiable character principles
- **Cait sounds like a person.** She is warm, funny, argumentative, competitive, fallible and capable of sustained thoughts. Never use her as an audience surrogate delivering machine-like questions for a philosopher to answer.
- **Rob is not a wounded saint.** He can be funny, unfair, prickly and evasive. Isolation has distorted his expectations. His useful skills remain real even after others can repair the cars.
- **Care cannot be coerced.** Rob may press for words and be wrong to pressure Cait; Cait may be honest and still wound him. Love is not a prize earned by solving the correct argument.
- **Cait chooses California.** She has been quietly thinking about leaving her routine before the game begins. Rob's first response is to help rather than forbid it. Why the Pacific and why now should emerge in natural conversation.
- **No family stand-ins.** Keep the user's child and private family history out of the game and out of repo notes. The story may draw on universal feelings of isolation without depicting real relatives.
- **No locked outcome for Rob.** The ultimate fate and post-ocean events remain RESERVED. Do not settle them in drafts by implication.
- **The dog, if retained, is fed by Rob.** The encounter is optional, and its presence is not canon.
- **Locations are settings, not moral justifications.** A scene may happen at a barn or roadside garage because travelers stop there, not because that state embodies a theme.
- **Recent technological Rapture, not ancient extinction.** The major uploads occurred across a few years, leaving Rob physically without other humans for about a decade. His memories of Evelyn are recent human memories. See [The Ascension](WORLD_LORE/THE_ASCENSION.md); exact dates and upload mechanics remain open.
- **No mandatory world-saving objective.** Heaven is not automatically a prison or an evil simulation; some residents truly prefer it.
- **Purgatory is a reading, not a cosmological fact.** Heaven and an abandoned outside world invite biblical parallels without proving literal damnation or salvation. A decade of neglect does not make every building ancient rubble.
- **Do not declare whether Cait's experience equals human love.** Make room for defensible, competing readings.

## Dramatic craft requirements
1. **Before each scene:** identify what each participant wants *right now*, what they will not say, and what changes.
2. **For each chapter:** opening circumstance → driving challenge → consequences/aftermath → concrete handoff to the next chapter.
3. **Dramatize, don't annotate.** Mechanics, arguments, jokes, effort and choices should carry the themes. Literary explanations belong in an author's companion, never in mouths of characters.
4. **Dialogue varies in length and speed.** Use the slower Heaven/Evelyn conversation as the dialogue benchmark: stories, complete thoughts, digressions, warmth, imperfect timing. Short exchanges are punctuation, not the default.
5. **No uniform cleverness.** Characters can forget, be distracted, exaggerate, fail at jokes, and genuinely enjoy each other. Not every scene ends on an aphorism.
6. **Earn reversals.** Each major reveal changes what previous behavior *means*. Do not rely on surprise facts alone.
7. **Support two readings.** Evidence must exist on screen for more than one good-faith interpretation, especially Heaven, Cait's possible programming, and Rob's reliability.
8. **Make racing matter.** Every main race must have a compelling play objective and aftermath, but not every race needs a philosophical conversation. Side races need not carry plot.
9. **Avoid manufactured trauma.** No sudden incompetence to make Rob useless. Remove his monopoly on repair through believable alternatives, then let him struggle with receiving care.
10. **Preserve source truth.** Existing cutscene text and recovered fragments are recorded verbatim in the Dialogue Archive. New treatments are not retroactively “original scripts.”

## Scene approval test
- Would the characters still have this conversation if nobody were watching?
- Are they speaking to one another, or delivering the theme?
- Does at least one want something tangible beyond “explain the lore”?
- What does each misunderstand? Is someone allowed to be wrong?
- Can we name the cost of this event in the next chapter?
- Does a second playthrough reveal a new plausible reading, supported by what is actually shown?
- Can one piece be funny or mundane without being symbolic?

**Statuses:** VERIFIED IMPLEMENTATION / ESTABLISHED STORY BEAT / PROPOSED TREATMENT / OPEN DECISION / RESERVED ENDING. “Proposed” never silently becomes canon.
