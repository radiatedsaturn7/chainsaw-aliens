# Motifs and Recurring Objects
**v0.1 · proposals, not required symbolism**

| Motif | First ordinary use | Later complication | What not to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **“Come back while you still can.”** | Radio warning during the Tower Hill storm | Heaven's invitations, a returning Cait, and the fear of a person who may not return | Repeat the line mechanically in every chapter |
| **The paper map to California** | Cait draws a route she chose herself | Detours, changed plans, crossed-out roads, and visible approach to the goal | Turn it into a magical plot device |
| **Rob's tools / a wrench** | Repairing cars, giving the journey practical shape | He uses work to avoid talk; others can repair; he must sit with no task | Imply physical work is worthless |
| **Unanswered transmissions** | Weak radio reception and distress signals | Rob's old attempts to reach people inside Heaven; missing Cait in a canyon | Every radio failure must mean abandonment |
| **Rain and snow** | Weather hazards for the driving game | Physical unpredictability versus Heaven's perfect simulation; Cait wants to *feel* the world | Explain the metaphor in dialogue |
| **Coffee / gloves / cassette tapes** | Unimportant shared habits and teasing | Cait notices small preferences and cares for Rob without asking for anything | Make each object carry a preassigned “lesson” |
| **“I love you” / refusal to say it** | An apparently ordinary wish for reciprocity | The other android can say it by routine, Cait cannot say it honestly, Rob hears absence despite visible care | Settle the ambiguity with one magic line |
| **The Ascension / Rapture-like imagery** | Recent public invitations to upload, deserted but still familiar houses, glowing facilities | The physical world may feel like purgatory to Rob, while uploaded citizens call their home Heaven | Turn allegory into a simplistic proof of sin, punishment or salvation |
| **The ocean** | Cait's concrete destination, apparently absurd because Rhode Island has one | A promise for her and both a gift and an emotional risk for Rob | Decide ultimate fate before final-act review |

**Subtext test:** For any recurring object, ask whether it can function credibly in the scene if the player never notices the symbolism. If not, remove the symbol or make the scene more ordinary.
