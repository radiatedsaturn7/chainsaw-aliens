# Last on the Grid — Narrative Blueprint
**Milestone 1 · v0.1 · 2026-11-17 · FOR REVIEW, NOT FINAL SCREENPLAY**

This document is the navigation hub. Read the [Narrative Constitution](NARRATIVE_CONSTITUTION.md), then the eleven [Chapter Treatments](CHAPTER_TREATMENTS/README.md). Character arcs, reveal map, motifs and the literary companion are linked below.

## One-paragraph treatment
In 2994, Robtergeist and Cait drive through an America whose cities and machines have mostly outlived human life. Cait has spent a long time following routines she never consciously chose. She wants to see the Pacific for herself; Rob, who finds his worth in being needed, agrees to take her. Along the route they encounter living creatures, unanswered signals, automated labor, uploaded human minds and other androids, each offering a competing picture of companionship. Cait's independence grows; the skills Rob uses to justify his place beside her become less essential. He wants her to say she loves him. She cares, demonstrably, but refuses a declaration she does not understand well enough to make. The trip tests both—not with a final lecture about love, but with choices that cannot be made for one another.

## What the player thinks the game is / what it gradually becomes
- **Act I (1–3):** A bleakly funny road trip with a mystery: where did everybody go, and why California?
- **Act II (4–7):** A world of unanswered calls and alternate lives. “Saving humanity” is revealed not to be the objective; obedience and intimacy are not equivalent.
- **Act III (8–10):** Cait gains real alternatives. Rob's mechanical indispensability fades. His need for words meets Cait's resistance to naming her experience; their conflict has consequences.
- **Act IV (11):** Arrival at the promised ocean. Fulfill the stated objective without resolving the reserved ending by accident.

## The *plot* that connects the encounters
1. **A real decision:** Cait has already decided to leave Rhode Island before Tower Hill. The player sees her commitment on the map the following morning; the direct account of her decision can be revealed in ordinary conversation, rather than interrupting the immediate playable opening.
2. **A promise and a practical route:** The next checkpoint is always grounded in fuel, road access, repairs, weather or a race opportunity. The Pacific is *Cait's* destination. Rob's commitment becomes both an act of care and an excuse to keep being useful.
3. **Signals and silence:** The Mount Washington distress repeater leads to hope and emptiness; an Ohio River signal awakens Rob's old habit of calling into the void. We learn the world is not devoid of all people, only of easy, mutual connection.
4. **What humanity chose:** Machine City shows purposeless service, then Heaven reveals a whole society that chose to live elsewhere. Evelyn's version of Rob's past differs in emphasis from his. Cait is offered an alternate life without her body.
5. **A question becomes personal:** The other scientist's obedient android can say “I love you” without choosing anything; Cait then chooses to save Rob *against his instruction*. Her ability to choose is evident, but it cannot conclusively settle where attachment comes from.
6. **Alternatives become real:** At Pikes Peak Cait races for fun, makes friends and may stay. The settlement's repair capabilities make Rob's contribution optional. His discomfort carries forward instead of being explained away.
7. **Costs and conflict:** A costly choice in Utah exposes how differently they understand responsibility. Rob's attempt to express love is not mirrored in the words he needs. In Arizona, Cait cares for him without needing anything repaired; Rob recognizes this and is furious that she still refuses to name it. Their quarrel produces an actual separation and return.
8. **The destination endures:** The goal does not become saving humanity, getting a romantic confession, or restoring Rob's usefulness. They still have a promise to keep. The final personal outcome remains an explicit open decision.

## What each chapter must deliver
Every treatment specifies (a) what *happens*, (b) what Rob wants, (c) what Cait wants, (d) what the player does, (e) how the closing scene changes the story, (f) the next-chapter consequence and (g) a second-reading possibility.

**Important:** These are *treatments*, not completed cutscenes. Except where implementation is verified or established dialogue is explicitly identified, lines and action are proposals.

- **Critical unresolved chronology:** The world is dated 2994, Rob is a biological human and may personally have known uploaded people before the exodus. A believable lifespan/history mechanism—or a different timeline—is needed before treating Evelyn's shared past with him as canon.
- **Critical unresolved geography:** Ozarks → St. Louis (eastward) must be motivated as a detour or the route reconsidered. The final Angeles Crest race does not itself reach the Pacific.
- **Critical unresolved characterization:** Exactly what Cait knew about her own creation and Rob's status before each conversation must be fixed prior to scripting mysteries.

## Recommended first review
Approve or rewrite the motivations, causality and chapter *order* first. Do not start by editing individual witty lines. After treatment approval, expand three calibration scripts: 01 (restraint), 06 (world and leisurely talk) and 10 (conflict). Only then draft all eleven.
