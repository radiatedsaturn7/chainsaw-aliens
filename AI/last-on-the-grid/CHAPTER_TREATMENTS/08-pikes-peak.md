# Mission 08 — Somewhere to Belong
**Pikes Peak Highway, Colorado · STRONG PROPOSAL, NOT APPROVED CANON**

## Narrative question
What does a relationship become when neither person's company is required by necessity? Can Rob celebrate Cait's happiness when he is no longer at its center?

## Beginning
Rob and Cait arrive at a functioning settlement of autonomous machines near Pikes Peak. These people have made a community rather than merely continuing a lost job: they disagree about racing regulations, play music badly, maintain a workshop, remember old wins, and have stupid rivalries. One local racer/engineer (name intentionally unset) takes to Cait. She has never had so many peers who know what it's like to inhabit machines and choose their own amusements.

Cait enters an uphill racing event because it sounds fun, not because somebody needs saving. Rob teases her about the local car, then helps with setup. A rare chance for **uncomplicated joy**: time to laugh and race and enjoy winning or losing.

## Playing it
A proper competitive ascent against machine racers on Pikes Peak, with identifiable rivals and post-race conversation reacting to what the *player actually did*. Strong driving identity matters. Cait's rival can admire her if she loses and needle her if she wins; script must accommodate both without rewriting the event's narrative meaning.

## Closing — two overlapping evenings
Cait spends hours with new friends debating gear ratios and boasting about a dangerous outside pass. Someone offers her a room and invites her to another race tomorrow. Rob has spent part of the day in an immaculate workshop where local technicians rebuild components in minutes and hand him perfect measurements. No one mocks his ability; he has simply become **unnecessary in the one role that helps him feel safe**.

The long after-race conversation should follow the voice benchmark. Let Cait talk excitedly at length; let Rob be mildly funny, not obviously tragic. A local calls her name from across the garage. She invites Rob to join them. He declines, then admits he saw more of her race than he let on. They are affectionate without trading declarations. He has something to lose, but she is not actually leaving.

## What each wants
Cait wants another race, friendship and possibly a few extra days, without having to apologize for wanting them. Rob wants to be included; his pride makes him seek technical tasks instead of accepting an invitation to simply hang out.

## Consequence / next chapter
They agree to continue toward the Pacific, perhaps after an extra day, but a new fact travels with them: Cait really could live elsewhere. And Rob has watched her be happy without his labor. He begins to misread ordinary invitations as opportunities to prove his usefulness. The strained dynamic carries into Moki Dugway, where the cost of a choice becomes tangible.

## Second reading
Rob's irritation is not because the machines are cruel. They're gracious. The wound is what he thinks it means when someone can do without him. Cait may have noticed his mood, but her excitement is not a betrayal.

## Drafting caution
Avoid writing a whole autonomous culture that exists solely to tempt Cait. The settlement should be fun and inconsistent, not a debate club. Do not let Rob's mechanical skill disappear—his lack of monopoly is what matters. Keep the possibility that Cait wants to stay **real**.
