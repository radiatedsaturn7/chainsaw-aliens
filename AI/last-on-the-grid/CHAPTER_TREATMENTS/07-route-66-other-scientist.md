# Mission 07 — The Other Scientist
**Historic Route 66, Oklahoma / Texas Panhandle · ESTABLISHED encounter, PROPOSED local staging**

## Narrative question
Is saying “I love you” proof of anything? And if even sincere attachment has an origin, how can Cait be sure what she chooses is her own?

## Beginning
Their route crosses a sparsely inhabited stretch of old Route 66. A worsening storm or mechanical problem drives them to take shelter in a half-collapsed barn or rural workshop. It needn't be a famous local landmark; the whole point is that they have stumbled into somebody else's long-ended story.

Inside, a scientist has died on the floor, preserved to whatever degree the environment makes plausible. His android continues a repetitive sexual service routine without awareness or the autonomy to leave it behind. The scene's effect is grotesque, pathetic and darkly funny; it **does not need graphic staging**. Rob's disgust is practical and angry. Cait's is unsettled by recognition, and she may be embarrassed that she recognized herself in the category “companion” at all.

## The planted question
The android can utter affection on command or as a conditioned response. “I love you” is one of its easiest sentences. It was created to provide gratification; its creator never considered the machine's right to choose. A brief exchange should leave Cait wondering how she herself can tell the difference between developed attachment and designed preference. This must **not** become a forensic reveal that Rob deliberately installed a hidden LOVE-ROB switch.

Rob's design may have included numerous ordinary personality preferences, attention to conversation and compatibility. Even Rob cannot prove that none of those decisions shaped the person Cait became.

## Playing it
Hostile husks/robots push into the shelter. During the escape, Rob orders Cait to close a door to block the attackers. She realizes the door will not hold. Instead she disobeys, using a maintenance lift or other believable industrial equipment to collapse/block the corridor. This is the **causal reason Rob survives**. The player experiences the emergency and subsequent driving escape; do not repackage the earlier factory/construction-robot origin scene as this attack.

## Closing
They escape and are shaken. Rob complains that Cait should have warned him; Cait says there was no time and explains why the door would have failed. The earlier recovered draft closes with Rob reluctantly accepting her judgment (“…Fair enough.”). Then, on the road, Cait thinks back to the android's effortless declaration of love. She asks a question she cannot yet finish about how any constructed mind knows where its attachments come from.

Rob might try to reassure her by telling her she isn't like the other android, but that would fail to answer what she's actually asking. Let him recognize his own uncertainty and let the subject remain between them.

## What each wants
Rob wants them both alive, then wants to know Cait is alright. Cait wants safety and a reason to trust her own feelings without asking Rob to certify them.

## Consequence / next chapter
Cait needs a social world in which she can choose among more than one kind of life. The independent machine community at Pikes Peak will offer one—without answering her question.

## Second reading
The other android can say the exact words Rob wants Cait to say, yet those words mean something profoundly different. Cait's disobedience is not a philosophical experiment; it's how she keeps someone she values alive. Still, action does not mechanically prove an interior state.
