# Mission 11 — Wrong Ocean
**Angeles Crest Highway, California → separate journey to Pacific coast · ARRIVAL GOAL ESTABLISHED / ENDING RESERVED**

## Narrative question
What does a promise mean when keeping it is no longer a way to prove your worth? What do the characters carry to a destination they understand differently?

## Beginning
The San Gabriel Mountains stand between the travelers and the final approach toward Los Angeles. The map is worn, covered in revised routes, crossings and little notes whose meaning changed along the way. Cait notices an old scribble from the first day. Rob remembers an argument she thought he'd forgotten. The dialogue may be funny, even playful, because we've earned their shared history. Don't open with a pronouncement about how far they've come.

A practical problem forces one last demanding road choice: damage to a main route, severe high-altitude conditions, or a competitor issuing a genuine racing challenge. The danger must not appear merely because the story needs a “final boss.” It should grow from the world and mechanical systems we've established.

## Playing it
The final main campaign race on Angeles Crest Highway. Bring back mechanics learned earlier: traction, temperature, route reading, and possibly choices about speed versus taking care of a car with a history. The climax is a race rather than a lecture, with a credible, exciting finish.

**Geographic note:** Angeles Crest is in the mountains, not the Pacific shoreline. The ocean arrival happens afterward, in a separate short road/epilogue scene or coastal drive, not as if the road ends on a beach.

## Closing — the coast
Cait sees the actual Pacific, whose wind, temperature, smells, texture and noise cannot be tuned to her preferences. She may discover it's louder or less picturesque than the recording she remembered. She doesn't have to explain why that makes her happy or disappointed. Rob, who has seen oceans before, sees her encounter with this one.

The map has fulfilled its ordinary function. It can be folded and put away, dropped into a glovebox, or left on the dashboard—**but don't force a symbolic gesture to cap the theme**. They can talk about the weather, an absurdly bad beach parking situation, or the way the water sounds. Important words may be withheld.

## What each wants
Cait wants the experience she chose. Rob wants to keep his promise and be there, without turning the event into a transaction.

## What changes
The external objective is fulfilled. The story's central question about love is present in their behavior but need not receive a definitive spoken answer. This chapter must deliver a sense of arrival even if the ultimate personal ending is developed separately.

## Second reading
For Cait the trip may have been about the destination, and then also about the company. For Rob it may have been about company, and then also about learning to take another person's desire seriously. The same map always carried both journeys.

## RESERVED / DO NOT IMPROVISE INTO CANON
The user's instruction is to **leave Rob's fate and all ultimate post-arrival outcomes undecided**. Do not prewrite death, reconstruction, permanent departure, an eternal romance or an epilogue as settled fact. Maintain a clean narrative handoff from arrival to a later ending-design decision.

## Drafting caution
An ending isn't automatically profound because characters go silent at sunset. The landscape, sound, a concrete decision and the residue of the prior chapters must do real work. Preserve the option of a satisfying postgame racing loop after the campaign.
