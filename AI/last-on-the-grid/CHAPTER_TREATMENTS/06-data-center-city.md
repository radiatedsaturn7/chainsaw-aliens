# Mission 06 — Heaven
**Data Center City, St. Louis, Missouri (fictional) · Major concept ESTABLISHED; Evelyn scene PROPOSED BENCHMARK**

## Narrative question
If a place can provide everything except the kind of reality you value, is refusing it a failure? Who abandoned whom when Rob stopped calling Heaven?

## Beginning
A city that appears deserted is still running, about a decade after most residents uploaded. Powered roads, cooled server towers, and well-maintained facilities stand beside mostly intact but darkening neighborhoods, storm-damaged roofs and neglected streets. They come to retrieve a component or use a safe passage; the network refuses unregistered vehicles, providing a reason for a driving challenge without turning the residents into villains.

Cait enters a terminal hall while Rob searches for a part. An apparently friendly older woman appears on a screen. **Evelyn** talks with the warmth, breadth and meandering memory of a person who has lived a long, full life. She is pleased to have a visitor. Cait can match her: jokes about cars, boredom, weather and what it is to have choices should sound like a conversation rather than a Q&A.

## Playing it
A tense run through service roads, cooling infrastructure and abandoned surface streets to reach an authorized loading bay or fix a local power path. Avoid a global “destroy Heaven” objective. Race conditions can include wet surfaces, coolant vapor, automated gate timing and resource management. This gives the city a distinct visual feel.

## Closing — inside
Evelyn explains the attraction of Heaven by telling stories about what she has actually done **since uploading only eight or nine years earlier**: places she's visited, painting she took up here, a simulated Florence that isn't crowded, and memories of friends from the short years of mass departure. She invites Cait to *leave her physical body and join*. Cait wants to know what it would cost to come back out. Evelyn is vague about why anyone would wish to. Her ability to offer **perfect rain** leads Cait to wonder why she wants rain that actually falls on her. Do not supply a scientific or philosophical lecture.

When Cait mentions Rob, Evelyn recognizes him. Evelyn and her husband Daniel knew him **before and during the few years of mass upload**, only about a decade ago. She remembers a stubborn, well-intentioned man who visited, tried to bring people back into the physical world, rejected their offers of a home inside, then stopped calling. Her recollection is fond, not accusatory. **The new chronology removes the old thousand-year lifespan problem.** Temporary visits into simulations, mind-copying and reentry remain OPEN.

## Closing — outside
Cait finds Rob loading the car. She mentions Evelyn and Daniel, who offered Rob a home overlooking a lake. He remembers the house and even its trees. He may have visited a simulation once; he didn't stay, though he couldn't say it was bad. Cait reveals she was invited in too. Rob struggles to sound unbothered. Their exchange eventually arrives at the restrained core: he tried to persuade people to come out; they didn't want to leave. Evelyn remembers *he* stopped contacting them. Rob admits he did. The truth is not that somebody is lying but that affection did not solve a profound difference in how they wanted to live.

## Additional conflict — the reverse Turing test [CORE MOTIVATION / SCENE PROPOSAL]
Heaven's engineered perfection is specifically why Rob rejected it: people he once knew seem *too* agreeable and conflict-free, like they are no longer themselves. Evelyn can be warm, funny and sincerely happy, but when Rob asks whether Daniel still thinks he was wrong to stay outside, she may offer careful reassurance rather than the actual disagreement. Rob's frustration is directed at the **loss of a recognizable voice**, not evidence that the uploaded are literally fake.

This also complicates Rob: he claims to value someone who will disagree with him, yet Cait's refusal to say “I love you” is the one honest disagreement he can't bear. The scene should plant this contradiction, not explain it.

## What each wants
Evelyn wants a guest to share a world she loves and perhaps reconnect with Rob. Cait wants to understand what she would be surrendering to gain that world. Rob wants to collect supplies and get back on the road without reopening old wounds.

## Consequence / next chapter
Cait now knows she could have an entire life separate from the trip and has heard a history Rob didn't narrate. A later encounter with a machine programmed for companionship will make the word “choice” far more disturbing.

## Second reading
Rob's “They didn't want to leave” is true. Evelyn's “He stopped calling” is also true. Asking who abandoned whom is a stronger debate than asking which of them is the villain.
