# Mission 04 — No Answer
**Ohio River Crossing, Marietta, Ohio · NEW PROPOSAL**

## Narrative question
What does unanswered communication do to someone who keeps reaching out anyway?

## Beginning
Their chosen crossing is blocked by aging machinery and damaged approaches. On the other side of the river a still-powered emergency relay emits a recognizable acknowledgment. Rob becomes unusually determined to reach it. Cait thinks he's trying to get useful bridge data. He lets her believe that.

They argue about how to reach the far bank, then drift into an absurd discussion of obsolete toll systems; the scene should breathe. Rob can be funny and frustrating, not a haunted silhouette in every shot.

## Playing it
A split route along the river and across a precarious alternate crossing. Avoid inventing a magical new survivor just to justify his hope. The relay can be automated, but its signal should plausibly be mistaken for an active endpoint until they inspect it. The player drives because a usable route is necessary; Rob's private hope is extra.

## Closing
At the relay hut, Cait brings power online. Rob hails the frequency, waits, retries with a different protocol. The automated acknowledgment comes back each time, identical. Cait realizes he understood its limitation before arriving.

Instead of confronting him with “You're lonely,” she begins to ask why he bothered. He fiddles with the old microphone and tells her about a terrible old broadcast he'd once heard. The story rambles, amusingly and a little pathetically. He never quite answers her question. She reaches over and leaves the receiver switched on while they eat.

**Candidate scene image:** A little green status LED blinking patiently in the dark while two people eat a lousy dinner in the next room.

## What each wants
Rob wants a voice to answer. Cait wants the route secured, and then wants to understand his stubbornness without making him feel examined.

## Consequence / next chapter
Data from the relay reveals a functioning industrial logistics corridor farther west. That becomes the route through the Ozarks and gives the next mission a practical reason to exist. The repeated call becomes a motif that will acquire a painful new meaning at Heaven.

## Second reading
Rob does not need a transmitter repaired; he needs a conversation to happen. The technical success is almost irrelevant to him.

## Drafting caution
Distinguish this from Mount Washington: there Cait insists on checking a *known automated distress call*; here Rob seeks the possibility of someone answering *him*. They should not feel like the same mission twice.
