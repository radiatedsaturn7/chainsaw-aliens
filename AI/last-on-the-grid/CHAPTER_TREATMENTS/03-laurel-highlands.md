# Mission 03 — A Stray (OPTIONAL)
**Laurel Highlands Scenic Byway, Pennsylvania · OPTIONAL dog encounter**

## Narrative question
What can care look like when nothing will come back to you? Can a scene be moving without either character identifying its lesson?

## Beginning
The map takes them through wintry Pennsylvania backroads; the following drive is a search for passage and supplies after the Mount Washington detour. Cait wants to make up lost distance. Rob wants to stop somewhere protected from the wind. Their petty discussion about whether a roadside farmhouse is even habitable can carry more life than a thematic setup.

A malnourished stray dog appears at a roadside property. Do not give the animal the mission's exposition or turn it into a collectible. **If we keep the scene, Rob is the one who feeds the dog.** Cait doesn't suddenly become a humanitarian lecturer. She might be practical: they need to be moving, food is limited, and the dog looks frightened.

## Playing it
Drive the Laurel Highlands stage through snow, deteriorated roads and farm country. The objective is to reach a usable route or supply site before the weather closes in. It must stand alone as satisfying racing. The dog encounter can come at the stop after the stage; it should not require a contrived “race for puppy food.”

## Closing
Rob searches their remaining food, quietly gives some to the dog, and waits several feet away rather than forcing contact. Cait watches as he struggles with a packet that won't open. They argue about how much is reasonable to leave. He does not take the dog, tame it, name it for an emotional climax, or demand gratitude.

As they drive away, Cait might look back and notice the animal eating. Rob has already turned his attention to the road. The scene should feel like a passing event in a long journey, not the trailer's designated “love without language” speech.

## What each wants
Rob wants the dog to get through another night and isn't wholly rational about the cost; Cait wants their supplies to last and is neither cruel nor wrong.

## Consequence / next chapter
The stop costs time or food. More important: the player has seen Rob caring when he cannot profit. In Ohio, he will seek a human voice with the same longing for contact but no guarantee of response.

## Second reading
The dog is not an allegory for a real child or a substitute for the plot. It offers a small contrast to Rob's own transactional view of worth; he can give without requiring usefulness from another creature, even as he struggles to receive the same.

## Open decision / replacement route
The user may **drop this scene entirely**. If cut, retain the Pennsylvania driving segment and use a different rest-stop interaction with a practical consequence; never reassign the dog-feeding moment to Cait by accident.
