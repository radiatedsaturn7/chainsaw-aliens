# Mission 09 — The Cost
**Moki Dugway, Utah · NEW PROPOSAL; hinge between companionship and confrontation**

## Narrative question
Who bears the cost of an independent decision when two people share a car, a route, and limited resources? Can a declaration of love survive being unanswered?

## Beginning
Rob and Cait leave Pikes Peak with a genuine disagreement still in the air. He is trying too hard to make himself useful; she is irritated that he treats a casual invitation as a request for a repair quote. On the way to the Dugway, they find an independent traveler stranded with a failed cooling system. There is a genuine danger to the stranger, but their own coolant supply is thin.

Cait insists they spare some. Rob doesn't want them stranded too. Let him propose another solution that costs more time, and let Cait believe time is the greater threat. **Neither should be written as morally stupid.** She chooses to give the coolant. It helps the stranger, but creates a real problem for them.

## Playing it
A steep, gravel switchback descent/ascent with an engine-temperature budget compromised by the choice at the start. The race tests mechanical management under pressure. The player should have interesting options, not simply suffer an unwinnable punishment because Cait was kind. Their route is hazardous, and a well-driven run can mitigate but not erase the cost.

## Closing — consequence
They get through, but repairs, lost time or an additional detour are necessary. Rob thinks she decided for both of them; Cait thinks she made the only decent choice. She has the right to decide, but not to pretend her choices don't affect another person. He has the right to be angry, but not to treat every disagreement as disobedience. No one gives the moral answer; they are tired, short of supplies and stuck sharing a campsite.

## Closing — the confession (PROPOSED POSITION)
Later, an ordinary evening turns unexpectedly tender. They listen to music while he sorts through objects he doesn't need to sort. Rob says, without grand build-up, “I love you.” Cait does not say it back. She tells him she cares but isn't certain she can honestly use that word for her own experience. Rob feels rejected, responds unfairly and leaves the conversation. **Do not make this a clean rational debate.** Let both say something they regret; the lack of resolution carries into Arizona.

The exact point where this confession occurs is not locked: late 08 or early 09 are alternatives. Wherever it lands, it must precede the full confrontation in Mission 10 by enough lived time to ferment.

## What each wants
Cait wants her moral agency recognized without being treated like a careless child. Rob wants his discomfort taken seriously and wants an assurance he cannot make her supply.

## Consequence / next chapter
Repairs and exhaustion create a credible condition where Rob needs rest and Cait does the fixing. His competence hasn't vanished; he cannot make himself the solution this time. Their unresolved argument ensures the care feels uncomfortable to both.

## Second reading
The stranger's survival is not the only measure of whether Cait made a good choice. And the confession is not just proof that Rob loves; it is also an invitation with an answer he is afraid of hearing.

## Drafting caution
Don't use the stranger as an elaborate moral lever. Give the person a voice or a personality, or cut them. Check that the mechanics do not punish players for a choice they weren't allowed to make.
