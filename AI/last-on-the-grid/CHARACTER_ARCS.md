# Character Arcs — Rob, Cait, and the People Between Them
**v0.1 · FOR REVIEW**

## Robtergeist — From indispensable to present
**Desire:** Be wanted. **Confused strategy:** Be too useful to abandon. **Fear:** “When I stop being useful, I'll stop mattering.” **Blind spot:** He can recognize Cait's care yet still insist on a verbal declaration, because the two forms of evidence do different emotional work for him. Neither his need for words nor Cait's refusal is trivial.

**Before the game:** A long period of isolation has made him difficult to reassure and slow to trust. His competence with machines is not theatrical; it has kept him alive. He loves repairing things partly because problems with parts have answers. He agreed to the Pacific because Cait wanted it, but the promise creates a role he understands.

**What the world does to him:**
- **01–02:** He worries, goes along with a detour, behaves like someone for whom coming home matters. He avoids admitting that he himself fears solitude.
- **03–05:** Helping for no return, unanswered radio calls, and pointless labor begin to make the player's portrait of him more complex. He does not explain it.
- **06:** Evelyn remembers him as a friend who was invited *in*. He remembers people refusing to come *out*. His isolation has a history, not a clean villain.
- **07:** His best instruction would have killed him; Cait's independent choice saves him. His utility and command are not always protection.
- **08:** He is a competent mechanic among machines that can do his work better and faster. No one devalues him; he devalues himself. Cait enjoys others' company.
- **09:** He reacts unfairly to a costly decision that affects them both, then speaks the words “I love you.” Cait does not answer in kind. He hears what isn't said.
- **10:** He is temporarily unable to do what he does best. Cait repairs the car and looks after him without a bargain. He *sees* her care and asks why she refuses to call it love. When contact breaks he assumes she has left; her return challenges the assumption without curing it.
- **11:** He has to keep a promise whose point was never to make himself indispensable. **What follows remains reserved.**

**Sign of change (not a final speech):** Cait invites him somewhere without asking for help. Instead of first negotiating what he can contribute, he sometimes chooses to go. No claim that his wound is permanently cured.

**Failure modes:** Rob never admits fault; every grievance turns out justified; his injury creates a magical new heroic skill; he becomes an abusive caricature; every silence carries a solemn moral.

## Cait — From inherited routine to lived preference
**Desire:** See, touch and experience a world she has only partly known; belong without being owned. **Fear:** Her attachment to Rob may have been built into her, perhaps without either of them noticing. **Blind spot:** She knows what she is trying to do for him, but may not realize how much his need for explicit words matters until she is confronted with it.

**Before the game:** She is already curious, capable, funny and competent. She has helped Rob and stayed near him for a long time. She has been thinking privately about how she fell into that routine without quite choosing it. An image or old recording of the Pacific gives her a personally chosen destination. Rain matters as a physical encounter with a world that does not automatically accommodate her.

**What the world does to her:**
- **01:** She insists on California, not on being “granted” agency. Rob helps.
- **02–03:** She pursues the distress signal and sees care expressed without guarantee of return; her reactions are her own.
- **04–05:** She is not merely Rob's translator of the world; she jokes, competes, argues and notices patterns he ignores.
- **06:** Heaven offers her endless experience and community if she leaves her physical body. Evelyn gives her a new account of Rob's history. Cait asks whether simulated rain is actual rain.
- **07:** An android casually says “I love you” as an obedience routine. Cait worries that her own affection may have been designed. Yet she disobeys to keep Rob alive. Neither evidence proves an origin story.
- **08:** She makes friends, has a brilliant day racing, and receives a real invitation to stay. She has more than one possible life.
- **09:** She chooses to help at a material cost, and resents that Rob treats independent choice as insubordination. She declines a declaration she cannot mean in the way Rob demands.
- **10:** She is frustrated by the discounting of the care she's *already* providing. Her “I've got the entire history of humanity on a chip…” speech is an angry defense of the fact that she is there because she wants to be, not because she needs a mechanic.
- **11:** Physical arrival at the ocean matters to her as an experience, not an essay about authenticity. **Her decision afterward remains reserved.**

**Sign of change:** She can stand by a choice and its consequences, acknowledge uncertainty without apologizing for existing, and maintain affection while refusing to surrender honesty.

**Failure modes:** Cait only asks questions; every choice she makes is morally correct; every argument makes her a cold diagnostic robot; she is secretly a programmed reward whose affection is explained away; her only interests are Rob's problems.

## Supporting character functions
- **Evelyn (Heaven) — strong proposal:** Warm, talkative and plausibly kind. She remembers Rob affectionately but also remembers that *he* stopped calling. She's not the game's mouthpiece or antagonist.
- **Independent machine racer / workshop friend (Pikes Peak) — proposal:** Give Cait a relationship that is not mediated by Rob; a peer with opinions and an inconvenient schedule, not an agent assigned to test their bond.
- **Other scientist's android — established encounter:** Obedient, capable of formulaic affectionate language, absent the power of independent refusal. Avoid sensationalizing it. Its speech can unsettle Cait without proving anything about her own subjective life.
- **Dog — optional:** If present, Rob feeds it; the scene must not turn into a lecture or a permanent pet-acquisition mechanic.

## The crossed misunderstanding
| Rob thinks | Cait thinks | Audience can watch |
|---|---|---|
| If I can't fix it, why would she bring me? | Why does he think every invitation is work? | Cait asks him to come; she invents no real requirement |
| If it's love, why can't she say it? | If I cannot verify that word, why should I promise it? | Cait keeps caring; Rob accurately sees it but needs language |
| She could stay with others; she won't need me | I have options, but I'm still traveling with him | Pikes Peak race, invitations, departure |
| Everyone in Heaven chose to leave me outside | We saw an invitation he refused as well | Evelyn and Rob each tell an incomplete history |

Do not reduce either to a single psychologically neat diagnosis. Contradictions are their character, not mistakes to be tidied up.
