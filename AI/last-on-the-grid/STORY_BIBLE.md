# Last on the Grid — Story Bible
> REVIEW DRAFT · 2026-11-13 · Not all entries are canon.

## Premise [ESTABLISHED]
Year 2994. Across a frozen, decaying United States after an AI catastrophe, Rob (a human) and Cait (an android) drive west from Rhode Island toward the Pacific. Rob promised Cait he would show her the ocean; after that, she is free to leave. Their journey is both a racing game and a story about loneliness, autonomy, love, mortality and what remains of humanity.

## Tone and storytelling [ESTABLISHED DIRECTION]
Restrained, character-driven dialogue. Let the world and silences communicate more than exposition. Racing and car customization must remain enjoyable independently of the narrative. Avoid dumping the biggest revelations in the opening act.

## Opening [ESTABLISHED]
1. Start in gameplay on snowy Tower Hill Road, Cumberland, Rhode Island. Cait retrieves fuel and must return as a storm shifts. The timed segment can be failed; either outcome proceeds to the garage.
2. Radio warning: “Hey… Winds shifted… Come back while you still can.”
3. Garage scene (current preferred dialogue):
   CAIT: “Got the fuel.”
   ROB: “Good.”
   CAIT: “Weather’s getting worse.”
   ROB: “Yeah. I know.”
   CAIT: “We still heading out tomorrow?”
   ROB: “That’s what I promised.”
   CAIT: “Right.”
4. Rob is soldering at a workbench. His pauses and reluctance matter more than an explanatory monologue.

## Main characters [ESTABLISHED CORE, DETAILS IN DEVELOPMENT]
**Rob** — Human mechanic/engineer, guarded, reluctant to revisit painful memories. He promised Cait the ocean. His reasons for creating Cait, and the precise nature of his isolation, are major story revelations. He is physically mortal.
**Cait** — Android, independent-minded, wants to see the ocean. She is not simply a passenger or a reward for Rob's kindness. She knows more than the player initially does; precisely what she knows and when is under review.

## Campaign spine [PLANNED, subject to change]
Middle Route major locations:
1. Tower Hill Road — Cumberland, Rhode Island (opening)
2. Mount Washington Auto Road — New Hampshire
3. Laurel Highlands Scenic Byway — Pennsylvania
4. Ohio River Crossing — Marietta, Ohio
5. Ozark Backroads — Missouri
6. Data Center City — St. Louis, Missouri (fictional)
7. Historic Route 66 — Oklahoma / Texas Panhandle
8. Pikes Peak Highway — Colorado
9. Moki Dugway — Utah
10. Oak Creek Canyon — Arizona
11. Angeles Crest Highway — California

The Pacific coast is the story destination, not necessarily the final listed race. Opening mission and later race durations remain design variables. Optional races, scrap economy, upgrades, and multiple US routes support replayability.

## Narrative beats [PROPOSED / DISCUSSED]
- Early encounter with a starving dog: Rob shares food, but the dog does not become a permanent companion. Demonstrates care beyond self-interest.
- Machine City: robots performing inherited work; Cait questions the human civilization that made them.
- Data Center City: uploaded humans, a synthetic “Heaven,” bodies/husks and machines attempting their own civilization. Rob has not joined them; exact reasons unresolved.
- Conflict: Cait presses westward, Rob resists or withdraws. His maintenance of the car can become a loaded argument, not an ownership claim.
- Ending concept: Rob dies near the destination or before Cait can return; Cait initially leaves, returns too late, and eventually tries to reconstruct him from surviving cells. A possible 10,000-year aftermath has been discussed. **This is a concept, not locked staging.**

## Key mysteries [UNRESOLVED]
- When does the player learn Rob is the last human alive?
- When does the player learn Rob built Cait to ease his loneliness?
- What exactly does Cait already know, and why does she want the ocean?
- Is Mount Washington an early boss, or should its emotionally revealing story move to Pikes Peak?
- How much of the original awakening/factory escape survives as backstory?
- How do bosses and upgrades support rather than interrupt the emotional narrative?
- What happens at the coast, and what remains playable afterward?

## Canon policy
**ESTABLISHED** = repeatedly accepted or explicitly chosen. **PROPOSED** = discussed without final commitment. **REJECTED/SUPERSEDED** = preserved for historical context, not active story. **UNRESOLVED** = needs a deliberate decision. Never promote a proposed beat to canon merely because it appears in this document.
