# Hempnettle 1955

Original badge-free 2994 interpretation of a 1955 European-specification Mercedes-Benz 300 SL W198 I steel-bodied road coupe, type 198.040: 2996 cc M198 I direct-injection straight-six, 8.55 compression, rear-wheel drive, four-speed manual and the listed 3.64 axle option. Excludes alloy-bodied 198.043, W194 racer, 300 SLR, modified competition cars and the later roadster.

## Manufacturer evidence

The German and English Mercedes-Benz public archive technical records are saved in full. German explicitly identifies **215 PS at 5800 rpm** and **28 mkg at 4600 rpm**. The English translation's “215 hp” is not treated as mechanical horsepower: runtime uses approximately 212.06 mechanical hp and 202.52 lb-ft, summarized as 212/203. No unsupported power test-standard suffix is added.

The 1295 kg mass follows the archive's DIN 70020 definition including fuel, spare wheel and tools. It is neither the 925 kg chassis mass nor gross weight; no driver is added. DIN 70020 is a mass convention here, not a claimed engine power standard. Production dates August 1954–May 1957 support the 1955 selection. The manufacturer's historical retrospective distinguishes the 29 alloy cars and later roadster suspension/brake changes.

Four forward ratios (3.34, 1.97, 1.39, 1.00), reverse 2.73 and selected 3.64 axle are sourced. Other listed axles are not mixed into the model or top-speed claim. Auto Assist is fictional assistance using the manual gears, not a factory automatic.

## Explicit modeling limits

The original tire designation is 6.50-15 Supersport on 5Kx15 steel rims. The editor's metric-only tire fields use an estimated 165/90-15 equivalent and 0.339 m rolling radius; this is not a claim that factory tires were modern radials. Weight distribution, steering ratio, CG/clearance, body colliders, overhang split, tire pressures/grip, suspension, brake/differential behavior, losses, torque interpolation, idle/rev limits, shifts and performance targets are estimates. Generic suspension does not reproduce swing-axle jacking/camber or drum-brake fade.

The archive's maximum of 260 km/h depends on rear axle ratio. The selected model's 240 km/h and 9.5-second 0–60 values are estimates, not tested factory claims. Doors stay closed. The fitted period-inspired metal/burgundy dashboard uses simplified live speed/RPM/gear/fuel with shared animated steering and tire art, plus existing generic sound.
