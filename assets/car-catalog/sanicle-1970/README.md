# Sanicle 1970

Local-only Legends tribute to Hans Herrmann and Richard Attwood's Porsche Salzburg 917 KH #23, chassis 917-023, overall winner of the 13–14 June 1970 Le Mans 24 Hours. Porsche confirms crew, victory and short-tail; ACO explicitly links chassis and race number 23. This is not the 1971 winner, a 4.9/5.0-litre evolution, long-tail, 956 or turbo Can-Am car.

## Source boundaries

Exact primary URLs and field-level provenance are in `import.json`.

- Porsche Christophorus's Attwood interview describes his chosen 4.5-litre short-tail with 500 bhp. That is the attributed reference used here. Porsche anniversary prose instead says 580 hp/PS; this conflict is disclosed rather than inventing an exact event dyno. No conversion of 580 PS into 580 bhp. The full curve and its 8,200-rpm peak remain estimates.
- ACO confirms the winning 1970 4.5-litre air-cooled 180-degree twelve, distinguishing 1971's 4,907 cc winner. Its separate early-capacity figure of 4,409 cc is not imported as an exact engine volume. Native 4.5L is the rounded reference, not a claimed measured bore/stroke. Generic sound is not an authentic twelve-cylinder recording.
- Attwood's firsthand interview, originally 2020 and republished by Magneto in 2026, explicitly confirms the four-speed gearbox and the instruction to use first only for starting. Four native forward gears are used. Ratios, final drive, losses, clutch and shift timings are estimates; start-only first gear is not mechanically enforced. Auto Assist is accessibility, not an original automatic.
- Running mass 900 kg, 340 lb-ft, 205 mph and 3.8-second 0–60 targets, tank 120L, 5 MPG, 300/50R15 nominal tire approximation, dimensions/geometry, brakes, suspension, steering and aero are estimates, not event measurements. No axle-specific historical tire claim or exact endurance consumption simulation.
